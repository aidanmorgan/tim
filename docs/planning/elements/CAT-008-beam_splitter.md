### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** The optical transport law carries a collimated segment to the pane; the Split response sends the declared fractions down two segments. Nothing is created: the two fractions sum to 1 in every channel. The part adds no law.
- **Half each.** Amber (10, 7.8, 3.2) W gives two segments of (5, 3.9, 1.6) W, broadband mean 10.5 / 3 = 3.5 W: each lights a default receiver (on at 2.5 W, which is 0.25 legacy units), 40 % above.
- **Quarter each.** A second splitter on one branch gives two segments of (2.5, 1.95, 0.8) W, mean 5.25 / 3 = 1.75 W: below the receiver's 2.5 W threshold and below its 2.25 W release level (0.9 × 2.5 W), so a default receiver reads a non-zero power and is dark whether or not it was lit before. The three receptions of the cascade are 0.5 + 0.25 + 0.25 = 1.0 of the source: 10.5 + 5.25 + 5.25 = 21 W.
- **Cone light.** A Flashlight cone is a terminal diverging segment: redirecting responses (Reflect, Relay, spread) apply to collimated segments only, so a Split never redirects a cone and makes no reflected branch of it. A diverging segment is multiplied by each Transmit surface between its origin and a sample and blocked by everything else ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions), item 3). A Split's transmitted fraction is such a surface: it multiplies a diverging segment by its τ, 0.5 in every channel, and the ρ share, which cannot be redirected, is absorbed by the pane and ledgered (not heat until ENGINE-THERMAL, decision K-10). So a sample seen through this pane receives half of each channel; a sample behind the frame or base is in shadow. (Item 3 states the Split case since its amendment of 23 November 2026.)
- **Direction.** With the beam along d = (1, 0, 0) and the pane turned +45° about +Z (right-handed), its −X face normal (−1, 0, 0) becomes n = (−0.7071, −0.7071, 0): the reflected ray is (1, 0, 0) − 2 × (−0.7071) × (−0.7071, −0.7071, 0) = (0, −1, 0), at 90°; the transmitted ray stays (1, 0, 0). Met from the other face (n seen as (0.7071, 0.7071, 0)) the same formula gives the same two directions: both faces behave alike.
- **Shared range.** Each branch inherits the parent's remaining range from where it starts: the reflected branch from the entry point, the transmitted branch from the exit point. The chord through the pane (0.03 m × √2 = 0.042 m at 45°) belongs to no segment and consumes no range; this is the reading of conventions item 5 that row R7 needs, raised to the conventions' owner (second audit X-7). A beam whose entry point is 6 m from the lens leaves 10 m on each branch: a receiver 9.5 m along either branch is reached and one 10.5 m along it is not.
- **Limits.** The split is one interaction on each branch's own path, so a path may meet at most 16 splitters and mirrors in all (row R10). All segments of one emitter together number at most 128 (requirement row), kept breadth-first by depth, then by emitter and surface order ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions), item 10). A splitter met square-on in a cavity doubles every ray it meets and keeps both halves in the cavity, so the cap is reached within a few metres of path (row R8).
- **The segment limit, worked (row R8).** Fixture, every part upright on one straight beam line at one height (static parts need no common floor); distances along the beam from the Laser's lens face: outer splitter S0 square-on, pane centre at 0.5 m; inner splitter S square-on, pane centre at 1.5 m; a Mirror (CAT-041) square-on facing the Laser, cap plane at 2.3 m (its origin at 2.48 m). Placement with the real part sizes, nothing interpenetrating: the Laser's base reaches 0.03 m past the lens but lies above the splitter base's height band (y −0.585 to −0.425 against −1.03 to −0.83), and its housing (y ±0.425, z ±0.40) and base (z ±0.5) fit inside the frame's 1.3 m opening; the two splitter bases, 0.9 m deep, span 0.05 to 0.95 m and 1.05 to 1.95 m (0.10 m apart); the Mirror's base front is at 2.48 − 0.45 = 2.03 m (0.08 m beyond S's base) and its backing (1.55 m square) stands behind its cap. Legs on the axis: lens to S0's near face 0.485 m; S0 to S 0.97 m; S to the Mirror's cap 0.785 m (pane faces ± 0.015 m). Every ray stays on the axis. S doubles each ray it meets and both halves stay in the cavity; S0 met from inside sends one half back into the cavity and the other onto the Laser, whose collar absorbs it (returning-ray occlusion, conventions item 6); the Mirror returns each ray at 0.95. Enumerated breadth-first by depth with the conventions' rules (each split halves every channel and each branch keeps the parent's remaining range; mirror retention 0.95; 16 interactions per path; a segment ends at range ≤ 1e-4 m or below 0.001 W total), the uncapped tree has, at depths 0 to 16, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 190 and 240 segments, cumulatively 1, 3, 5, 8, 12, 18, 26, 38, 54, 78, 110, 158, 222, 318, 446, 636 and 876. The cumulative count passes 128 at depth 11 (158), where every path is at most 0.485 + 11 × 0.97 = 11.2 m long and has used 11 of its 16 interactions: neither the 16 m range nor the interaction limit has stopped growth, and uncapped the tree would stop only at depth 16 (876 segments, ended by the interaction limit and the 0.001 W cut-off; range ends none of them). The cap therefore binds at depth 11: all 110 segments of depths 0 to 10 are kept and 18 of the 48 at depth 11, chosen by emitter and surface order; none deeper is made. The weakest kept segment carries 21 W ÷ 2¹¹ = 0.0103 W in total (depth 11, every interaction a split), ten times the 0.001 W cut-off, so no kept segment is lost to it. The counts to depth 11 depend only on the order of the surfaces along the axis, not on the leg lengths, so a 0.02 m pose error cannot change them. Control, S removed: the beam rattles between S0 and the Mirror (1.785 m legs), losing one half to the Laser at each return to S0; 15 segments, the last ending at the 16 m range 0.58 m short of the next surface.
- **One interaction per split, worked (row R10).** The Laser's lens 3 m back along the entry line of the Mirror spec's sixteen-reflection corridor (CAT-041, Behaviour and numbers, where the lens is placed the same 3 m back), with this part square-on to the beam midway, 1.5 m from the lens and 1.5 m before the corridor's entry crossing. The transmitted branch, (5, 3.9, 1.6) W, enters the corridor having used one of its 16 interactions, so it reflects 15 times and its sixteenth hit, on mirror B's reflecting aperture, ends the path; the reflected branch returns along the incoming line and ends on the Laser's collar. Range: 3.0 m to the entry crossing and 9.85 m for the corridor's first sixteen segments, 12.85 m of 16 m, so range cannot end the path first. Power after 15 reflections: 10.5 W × 0.95¹⁵ = 4.9 W in total, far above the cut-off. Placement: square-on to a beam 17.2° from mirror A's normal, the frame's end nearest mirror B lies about 1.6 m behind B's cap plane and the base's nearest corner 1.25 m behind it, clear of B's backing (0.31 m behind its cap) and base (0.63 m behind); the splitter's base ends 1.0 m before the Laser's.
- **Whole pane in a square opening.** The pane fills the 1.3 m square opening (half-width 0.65 m). With the splitter square-on to the beam (not turned), a ray 0.74 m above the pane centre meets the top rail's near face (rail 0.65 to 0.83 m) and is absorbed. In the 45° base fixture a beam moved by (−0.354, 0.354, 0.60) m meets the pane at x = −0.354 m, pane-local (y 0.50, z 0.60) (y = 0.7071 × 0.354 + 0.7071 × 0.354 = 0.50): 0.78 m from the centre, outside the printed ring (outer edge 0.65 m), 0.15 m inside the top rail and 0.05 m inside the post; it is split like any other. ("Raised 0.74 m" in the turned fixture has no single meaning: there the beam passes beside the rail.)
- **Solid pane.** The pane is a box collider: every ball stops at it.
- **Tick order.** A splitter holds no state. One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. The splitter splits at its pose at the end of the tick's substeps. No optical phase is delivered today.

**Tolerances** (the optical family's one set; coordinator decision 21). Power readings ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 or 0.5 factor is at least 10 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Reflected direction 0.5°: a beam aimed at the centre of a 0.55 m target misses at the 16 m range beyond atan(0.55 ÷ 16) = 1.97°, and 0.5° is 0.14 m at 16 m. Segment end points ± 0.02 m: under 5 % of the smallest aperture radius (0.43 m), so a path that would clip or miss an aperture, or a branch that ends at the wrong range, fails. Threshold, edge and range cases are placed either side of the decision (R4's 3.5 W and 1.75 W about 2.5 W; R5's 0.60 m and 0.74 m about the 0.65 m opening; R7's 9.5 m and 10.5 m about 10 m): positions of designed cases, not tolerances; those rows assert state only. Exact: segment, per-depth and reflection counts (declared limits), authored Reset values and the absence of any segment or reading after Reset (intentional zero). No row sits within 2 % of a threshold. R6's ball stop is a contact clearance (its centre at least its radius less 0.02 m short of the pane) with 5 s of game time as its stall guard.
