### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** Optical transport delivers channel power to the aperture; the receiver rule turns the reading into a committed strength (zero when purity fails); the threshold signal node turns the strength into the level `matched`; the supply network's contact law passes whatever supply reaches `PowerIn` to `Supply` while `matched` is on. The part adds no law of its own.
- **Green and blue together.** A green laser (0, 10, 0) W and a blue laser (0, 0, 10) W on the target read (0, 10, 10) W: strength 10 W ≥ 2.5 W (0.25 legacy units), purity 100 %, matched. The same two lasers merged by a Beam combiner arrive as (0, 9, 9) W: matched.
- **Filtered amber through a combiner.** Green-filtered and blue-filtered amber merged by a Beam combiner arrive as (0, 7.8 × 0.9, 3.2 × 0.9) = (0, 7.02, 2.88) W: strength min = 2.88 W, 15 % above the default, purity 100 %, matched. One mirror after the combiner leaves blue at 2.88 × 0.95 = 2.736 W (9 % above). Three mirrors leave 2.469 W, 1.2 % under the threshold and inside the hold band (2.25 W to 2.5 W): a dark receiver stays dark and a lit one would hold, so no row uses that case. A Beam splitter after the combiner leaves 2.88 × 0.5 = 1.44 W: dark, 42 % below the threshold and 36 % below the release level. Blue is the weak channel: a filtered-amber cyan path affords two mirrors at most, and the lasers are the robust sources.
- **One required channel missing, or red alone.** Green only (0, 10, 0) W: B = 0, dark. Blue only (0, 0, 10) W: G = 0, dark. Red alone (10, 0, 0) W: both zero, dark.
- **Amber.** (10, 7.8, 3.2) W: both required channels pass the threshold, but purity (7.8 + 3.2) / 21 = 52.4 % < 90 %: dark. The red part, 10 W, is eight times the limit (11 / 9 = 1.22 W).
- **Per-channel boundary.** With blue at 10 W and the green laser through one splitter, G = 5 W: lit. Through three splitters, G = 1.25 W: dark, 44 % below the 2.25 W release level, although blue is four times the threshold. The same holds with the colours exchanged. (Two splits give exactly 2.5 W, so no row uses them.)
- **Purity limit.** Matched needs the red channel to be at most (G + B) / 9. With (0, 10, 10) W the limit is 20 / 9 = 2.22 W. Red-filtered amber (10, 0, 0) W added through three splitters is 10 / 8 = 1.25 W: purity 20 / 21.25 = 94.1 %, matched (44 % under the limit). Added through one splitter it is 10 / 2 = 5 W: purity 20 / 25 = 80 %, dark (5 W is 125 % over the limit). Two splitters (2.5 W, purity 20 / 22.5 = 88.9 %) are not used: 2.5 W is the two-split value the family keeps out of its rows. Purity has no release band: it is tested on every tick, and strength is zero in a tick where it fails.
- **Threshold range 0.5 to 20 W.** At 0.5 W both laser beams still light it after four splits each (0.625 W) and not after five (0.3125 W, below the 0.45 W release level too). At 20 W, three beams of each colour light it (30 W each) and one of each does not (10 W, below the 18 W release level). Six colour lasers ask 6 × 12 = 72 W of the default Battery's 120 W, so each emits at full power.
- **Release ratio 0.9** (decision K-4 of [readiness/batch-k.md](readiness/batch-k.md): a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact). A matched receiver stays matched until its strength falls below 0.9 × `threshold`; a dark one needs the full `threshold`. With `threshold` 10.5 W the band runs from 0.9 × 10.5 = 9.45 W to 10.5 W, and one green and one blue beam, (0, 10, 10) W, lie inside it: 4.8 % below the threshold and 5.8 % above release. Two beams of each colour, (0, 20, 20) W, turn the receiver on; when one pair stops, the remaining (0, 10, 10) W holds it; from dark, that pair never turns it on.
- **Cone light.** One receiver law serves every kind of light (owner decision 9). The Flashlight emits (30, 30, 30) W over a 15° half-angle: solid angle Ω = 2π(1 − cos 15°) = 0.2141 sr, so 90 / 0.2141 = 420.4 W/sr in total and 140.1 W/sr per channel. With its lens 3 m in front of the disc on the disc's axis, facing it, the irradiance is 420.4 / 3² = 46.7 W/m²; the disc's share is 0.950 m² × 46.7 W/m² = 44.4 W, 14.8 W per channel (it is the only receiver in the cone and 44.4 W is below the 90 W emitted, so no share is scaled). Green and blue are each 5.9 times the threshold, but purity is 29.6 / 44.4 = 66.7 %: strength zero, dark (row R11). A laser row is never proof of torch reception ([sequence-task-398](../requirements.md#sequence-task-398)).
- **Finite disc.** A ray 0.50 m off-centre is read; one 0.60 m off-centre meets the plate. Both beams must land inside the disc.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The switched output feeds any `PowerIn`; the proof load is the Powered gate. A contact consumes no power, so the Battery's 120 W default limit is unaffected by the receiver; the two lasers draw 12 W each. The 0.26 m plate is a wall to every ball.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. No optical solve is delivered today. The strength committed with one tick is read by the node `matched` at the start of the next, so light closes the supplied contact one tick later, as a delivered state-following contact does (`LatchSupplyFixtureTests.SetClosesRouteOneTickAfterStrikeWithoutTopologyChange`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/LatchSupplyFixtureTests.cs:145`: the route closes one tick after the state sets). All readings of a tick commit together, so the green and blue contributions of a tick are never read apart. This is the requirement's "trace every emitter, then commit all receiver readings together before the electrical solve" ([todo-290](../requirements.md#todo-290)), where the solve is the one that consumes them.

### Engine conformance (22 November 2026)

Checked against the owner's engine-conformance instruction ([element-free engine design](../../../_bmad/custom/element-free-engine-design.md); AGENTS.md, Tri-Graph architecture). A search of `engine/`, `ui/` and the `CuriousContraptions.*` runtime projects for the 22 Batch E package ids found no reference; only `tools/Coverage` requirement tables name them. Verdict: **Conforms**: the declaration needs no element logic and every missing capability has one owning element-free story.

1. **Declaration data only.** A scene, four colliders, one aperture, one receiver rule, one threshold signal node, one contact node, two sockets, one parameter, three bindings. The legacy channel `switch`, per-part reception callback and parameter dictionary (harvest facts 2, 4 and 5) are not carried; "cyan" is declared data (the required set {G, B} and a purity fraction), never an engine branch on a colour or a part.

Each is a general capability any package may declare; the eight receivers are one rule with eight sets of values.
3. **Three layers.** Colliders: two boxes and two spheres on the static body; nothing in physics reads art. Visuals: four solids and eight flat prints; no art node is a collider. Animation: three colour bindings read the committed signal level `matched` and write nothing back.
4. **One-way flow and typing.** Optics → committed reading and strength → the signal node at the start of the next tick → the supply contact; committed state → presentation. No contact reads the receiver directly. `PortId`, `PortRole`, `SimulationDomain` and the planned response, aggregation and channel-set types are typed; all values are f32.
5. **Flat state.** Bodies 1 (static); colliders 4 (box 2, sphere 2); joints 0; drives 0; receivers 1 (one aperture, one sample point); signal nodes 1, supply nodes 1 with one electrical rating row; stores 0; presentation bindings 3. All counts are fixed at compile time.

**Tolerances** (the optical family's one set; coordinator decision 21). Power readings, beam or cone, ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 or 0.5 factor is at least 10 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Threshold cases are placed 5 % either side of the reading: these are positions of designed cases, not tolerances, and those rows assert state only. Exact: typed refusals, authored Reset values and colour endpoints, and a zero reading where no light reaches the aperture (declared limits and intentional zero). No row sits within 2 % of a threshold, and no contaminant within 2 % of its purity limit.

**Covered generically.** Lifecycle of an unlit instance: P4. Wrong-kind wires: the wire law of P3; no row of its own. The art table row: P2.

## Open questions

All resolved. Needs the owner: none.

1. **Meter — decided 22 November 2026 under the standing delegation:** the eased target is the indicator the requirement row's "meter/indicator" names; no separate meter is added. Reason: DESIGN.md gives receivers the slate-to-gold disc and nothing else, a threshold part has one bit to show, and R10 proves the indicator and the switched output agree (D19).
2. **Threshold range — decided:** 0.5 to 20 W, default 2.5 W, as every receiver in the family (decision K-1: one legacy unit is 10 W), applied to each required channel. Reason: [todo-301](../requirements.md#todo-301) says "every named channel at threshold"; one range for one rule keeps the family a family (D19).
3. **Tick order — coordinator decision 16; conventions item 4.** The order is in Behaviour and numbers.
4. **Proof load — decided:** the Powered gate (delivered, takes `PowerIn`, visible response).
5. **Mixing — decided:** two beams landing on the target add, with or without a Beam combiner. Reason: the reading is the sum of all absorbed light (harvest fact 6).
6. **Pure sources.** Their radiant power, 10 W in one channel for a demand of 12 W, is the value of EL-176 to EL-178 and of the [optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions).
7. **Decided 22 November 2026 under the standing delegation.** (a) Target, ring, centre and channel bars are flat prints; reason: visible = solid, and a relief of a few centimetres changes no puzzle outcome. (b) The base is raised 0.09 m and the sockets move to the plate's side faces; reason: the legacy geometry left a floating plate and buried studs. (c) One bar format for the family (0.08 × 0.04 m, rows 0.05 m apart, centred on y 0.625); reason: the legacy rows overhang the plate top and overlap the target once printed flat. (d) Release ratio 0.9 (decision K-4): `matched` turns off below 0.9 × `threshold`; reason: a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact. (e) Palette order 617 (coordinator decision 36). (f) The dark purity case of R5 uses one splitter (5 W of red, purity 80 %), not two (2.5 W, 88.9 %); reason: 2.5 W is the two-split value the family keeps out of its rows.
8. **Flashlight cones — owner decision 9.** One receiver law serves all light: a diverging segment adds its conserved share A × Σ wᵢEᵢ to the reading (conventions item 8). An unfiltered torch fails purity here (row R11).
9. **Coupling — coordinator decision 16; conventions item 9.** Committed strength → the threshold signal node `matched` → the delivered signal-state contact. No contact reads the receiver.
