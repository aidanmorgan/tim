### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** Supply: the consumer asks the network for 25 W; the delivered reservation law grants it against the source's per-phase allowance, in full or as a proportional share. Enable: the signal memory law. Emission and transport: the emitter and segment laws of the conventions. On each tick on which `enable` is set the emitter sends one collimated segment of (10, 7.8, 3.2) W × (energy granted over the tick's substeps ÷ energy demanded); a tick with no grant over its substeps, or with `enable` clear, has no segment.
- **Radiant power (10, 7.8, 3.2) W** (requirement row "authored amber linear-RGB power": the legacy (1.0, 0.78, 0.32) at 10 W per unit). Sum 21 W, broadband mean 21 / 3 = 7.0 W. It lights a default broadband receiver (7.0 W ≥ the 2.5 W threshold), survives one 50 : 50 split (3.5 W ≥ 2.5 W) and not two (1.75 W, also below the 2.25 W release level), and fails every 90 % purity rule (red share 10 / 21 = 47.6 %), which is the intended filter lesson (decision D18).
- **Range 16 m** (requirement row). A receiver whose target is 15.9 m from the lens is lit; at 16.1 m it is dark. The range is one budget shared by every later segment of the path.
- **Demand 25 W** *proposed* (conventions, item 2). 25 W × dt = 0.2083 J per tick at the default 1/120 s tick. The default Battery allows 120 W, 1 J per tick at the default tick (`elements/Battery/BatteryElementDeclaration.cs:44`), and holds 3600 J: one Laser runs 3600 / 25 = 144 s. Radiant 21 W ≤ 25 W demanded: the 4 W difference is the declared loss and never becomes light, so emitted energy is 84 % of the energy paid. Four Lasers on one default Battery ask 4 × 25 = 100 W ≤ 120 W and all run at full power. Five ask 125 W; the delivered reservation law shares the allowance in proportion (`ElectricalSupply.Reserve`, `engine/gpu/ElectricalSupply.cs:11`, proved only as a pure function by `ElectricalSupplyTests.UnequalDemandSharesProportionallyAndFullLoadReceivesNothing`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/ElectricalSupplyTests.cs:207`: with 1 J available and demands of 1 J, 3 J and 0 J the grants are 0.25 J, 0.75 J and 0 J; probe row R7), so each is granted 120 / 125 = 96 % of its demand (0.2 J per tick) and emits 96 % of its radiant power, (9.6, 7.488, 3.072) W, mean 6.72 W, which still lights a default receiver. The five together emit 5 × 21 × 0.96 = 100.8 W of light for 120 W paid. At the Battery's minimum `power_limit` of 10 W one Laser is granted 10 / 25 = 40 % and emits (4, 3.12, 1.28) W, mean 2.8 W.
- **Supply loss.** A tick with no grant over its substeps has no segment: the beam is gone at the next optical snapshot, as the requirement says. `enable` is signal memory and is untouched; when supply returns the beam returns with no new trigger. The delivered parallel is the Impact switch's latch surviving a supply interruption (`SwitchElectricalTests.SupplyLossAndRestorationDoNotEraseOrReplayImpactMemory`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/SwitchElectricalTests.cs:72`); no delivered test has a latch enabling an electrically paid consumer, so rows L1 to L3 are the first proof of a latch-enabled wired emitter (probe row R5).
- **Own geometry.** The first segment starts on the collar face and skips the emitter's two declared internal occluders (housing and collar). Every later segment meets all five colliders as ordinary occluders with the Absorb response: a reflected or relayed ray that returns ends on the Laser.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The trigger is any Pulse output: Impact switch, Delay box, Ball detector, System clock, Counter. The supply is a Battery or any switched `Supply`. The housing is a solid obstacle 0.85 m tall.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. For this part: a tick with no grant over its substeps has no segment, and a pulse that sets `enable` during tick n is resolved at the start of tick n + 1, so emission begins with that tick's solve. No optical solve is delivered today.

### Engine conformance (22 November 2026)

Checked against the owner's engine-conformance instruction ([element-free engine design](../../../_bmad/custom/element-free-engine-design.md); AGENTS.md, Tri-Graph architecture). A search of `engine/`, `ui/` and the `CuriousContraptions.*` runtime projects for the 22 Batch E package ids found no reference; only `tools/Coverage` requirement tables name them. 3. **Three layers.** Colliders: two boxes, one cylinder, two spheres on the static body. Visuals: five solids and one flat print; the beam is the renderer's. Animation: three colour bindings read the committed emission and write nothing back.
4. **One-way flow and typing.** Supply grant over the substeps → optical solve after the last substep → committed segments with the tick's poses → presentation; the signal network reads its levels at the start of a tick and the emitter reads them. This part adds 1 to N and no receiver sample point. A segment ends at 1e-4 m of range or 0.001 W. The owning performance story states a measured before and after on a fixed reference scene; the tick target is confirmed at the performance gate. This spec designs around no performance gap.
7. **Tests are scenarios.** Rows L1 to L11 run the production simulation; none depends on a laser-specific code path.

**Tolerances** (the optical family's one set; coordinator decision 21). Power readings ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 or 0.5 factor is at least 10 %; a wrong factor, or a beam that ignores its grant (4 % dimmer in L8), flips a detector or fails the row. Energy debited over a window ± 2 %: the debit is demand × window, and the one-tick edge at either end is 0.17 % of a 5 s window (0.83 % of a 1 s window), so a debit outside 2 % is unpaid or overpaid light, not timing; emitted energy (21 W) is at most the debit (25 W paid), a 16 % margin that no tolerance can close. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Direction 0.5°: a beam aimed at the centre of a 0.55 m target misses at the 16 m range beyond atan(0.55 ÷ 16) = 1.97°, and 0.5° is 0.14 m at 16 m. Segment end points ± 0.02 m: under 5 % of the smallest aperture radius (0.43 m). Lens colour within 1/255 of each authored endpoint: one 8-bit display step. The range cases (15.9 m and 16.1 m about 16 m) are positions of designed cases, not tolerances; those rows assert state only. Exact: counts (one `MemoryChanged` record), no segment where no emitter is active, authored Reset values and typed refusals (declared limits and intentional zero).
