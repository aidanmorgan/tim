### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** The optical transport law carries each collimated segment to an inlet disc; the Relay response ends it there and starts a segment at the outlet with the declared retention, deducting the declared internal path from its range. Light is neither made nor mixed inside: the "combination" is several segments leaving one point in one direction. The part adds no law.
- **Retention.** Red-filtered amber (10, 0, 0) W leaves as (9, 0, 0) W. Three Lasers through red, green and blue filters, one into each input: (10, 0, 0) + (0, 7.8, 0) + (0, 0, 3.2) → × 0.9 = (9, 7.02, 2.88) W at a receiver on the output; 21 W reaches the three inlets, 18.9 W leaves the outlet and 2.1 W is absorbed in the cube (ledgered; not heat until ENGINE-THERMAL, decision K-10). A White receiver's strength is the smallest channel, 2.88 W ≥ 2.5 W (0.25 legacy units), 15 % above the default threshold and above its 2.25 W release level. The three Lasers ask 3 × 25 = 75 W of the default Battery's 120 W, so each is granted in full.
- **No synthesis, no duplication.** Red and green only: (9, 7.02, 0) W; the blue reading is zero. In every channel the output is 0.9 × the sum of the inputs, never more.
- **Cone light.** A Flashlight cone is a terminal diverging segment: redirecting responses (Relay among them) apply to collimated segments only, and a diverging segment is blocked by every surface that does not declare Transmit ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions), item 3). This part therefore only occludes a cone and its inlets relay none of it.
- **Range, worked.** Laser lens 4 m from the First input: 16 − 4 = 12 m remain at the face; the relay deducts 1.40 m; the output segment has 10.6 m. A receiver 10 m beyond the outlet is reached and one 11 m beyond is not. Through the Second input at the same distance the ray has 16 − 4 − 0.99 = 11.01 m.
- **Dies inside.** Laser lens 15 m from the First input: 1.0 m remains, less than the 1.40 m to the outlet. The input is reached, nothing exits and the output lens stays slate. At 14 m, 2.0 m remains and a ray exits with 0.6 m to go.
- **Finite inputs.** A beam 0.60 m from an input's centre is outside its 0.43 m disc and inside the 0.70 m half-face: it meets the cube and stops. The −Z and −Y faces have no input and stop every beam.
- **Loops.** If three mirrors return the output to an input, each lap multiplies the beam by 0.9 × 0.95³ = 0.772 and spends range and four of the path's 16 interactions; the path ends when either budget is spent. Worked: Laser lens 1 m from the First input, a 6 m external lap from the outlet to the Third input. At the outlet 16 − 1 − 1.40 = 13.6 m remain; the first re-entry arrives with 13.6 − 6 = 7.6 m and leaves the outlet with 7.6 − 0.99 = 6.61 m; the second re-entry arrives with 0.61 m, less than the 0.99 m internal path, so it ends inside the cube. Exactly two re-entries, using 9 of the 16 interactions: range, not the interaction limit, ends this loop.
- **Interaction count.** The relay counts as one interaction. Fed into the sixteen-reflection corridor of the Mirror spec (CAT-041, Behaviour and numbers) along that corridor's entry line, a beam that has passed this part once has 15 interactions left: it reflects 15 times and its last segment ends on mirror B's reflecting aperture, where a sixteenth reflection would otherwise occur. Range: Laser lens 2 m from the First input, 1.40 m internal, the outlet 1.5 m before the corridor's entry crossing, and 9.85 m for the corridor's first sixteen segments: 14.75 m, inside 16 m.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The 1.4 m cube is a wall to every ball and to the pipe bore, 1.3 m across.
- **Tick order.** The combiner holds no state between ticks. One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. The outlet's exiting power commits with the readings; presentation reads the committed value.

**Tolerances (the one optical set; coordinator decision 21).** Power readings ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 factor is 10 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. "Zero" in a channel is below 2 % of the source (0.2 W), so a synthesised channel fails. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Segment end points ± 0.02 m: under 5 % of the smallest aperture radius (0.43 m), so a path that would clip or miss an aperture fails. Exact: interaction and re-entry counts (declared limits), authored Reset values and the absence of any segment or exiting power after Reset (intentional zero). Positions placed either side of an edge or a range (0.40 and 0.60 m about the 0.43 m radius; 1.0 and 2.0 m about the 1.40 m internal path) are designed cases, not tolerances; those rows assert state only. The settled lens colour is compared ± 0.02 per component, the colour equivalent of 0.3 W in a channel (0.0667 per watt), so a lens driven by the entering instead of the exiting power (10 % more, 0.06 in red) fails.
