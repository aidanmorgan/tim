# CAT-065 · trampoline declaration readiness spec

This is the CAT-065 spec for the Trampoline. Sections 1 to 3 and 5 to 9 describe the delivered package and the engine law it uses, checked on 22 November 2026 against checkout `8d1d68aa`; published main is now `25df3cd5`, and its two later commits touch none of this package's files. Section 4 is the legacy harvest at `a6c914e`, kept verbatim as evidence; it is not a description of the current part. Visual authority is the [Passive trampoline](../../../DESIGN.md#passive-trampoline) section of DESIGN.md. Browser qualification is pending until the post-catalogue phase.

**The part does not conform to the engine design as delivered** (section 9). The package is declaration data, but the engine law it relies on carries this product's ranges in shared admission and does scene-proportional work every substep. The repair is split by clause across the engine programme (owner decision 3a, 22 November 2026): broadphase-bounded sampling under `ENGINE-SPATIAL-QUERIES`; the one-surface-per-element slot and the dense surface × body reservation under `ENGINE-CAPABILITY-INSTANCES`; bed friction and rolling resistance under `ENGINE-COMPLIANT-FRICTION`. The remaining clauses (admission bounded by the law's stability condition instead of product ranges, a per-sample committed deflection read, and the height-field sag binding) are also `ENGINE-COMPLIANT-FRICTION`'s (coordinator decision 29).

### Bodies and shapes
**Frame (`elements/Trampoline/trampoline.tscn`).** One static frame body built as DESIGN.md's passive trampoline; every visible frame part collides. In metres, in the part frame:
- navy back plate at (0, −0.51, 0), size 2.72 × 0.12 × 2.12. Its top face is the stroke floor −0.45 = rest height − maximum stroke, so it stops the stroke;
- two cream side rails at (±1.28, 0.1, 0), size 0.16 × 0.2 × 2.12, and two cream end rails at (0, 0.1, ±0.98), size 2.4 × 0.2 × 0.16. Their tops are the rest plane 0.2 and their inner faces the footprint edges x = ±1.2 and z = ±0.9;
- four gold corner posts, cylinders of radius 1/16 m (0.0625) and 0.45 tall, at (±1.28, −0.225, ±0.98): each stands on the plate's top face and reaches the rails' underside, outside the footprint (inner extents 1.2175 > 1.2 and 0.9175 > 0.9), so no post intrudes into the stroke volume. The radius is 1/16 m, the engine's minimum cylinder radius, drawn and colliding identically;
- four gold feet, cylinders of radius 0.07 and height 0.2 at (±1.2, −0.67, ±0.9), below the plate;
- the sides stay open between the rails (underside at 0) and the plate (top at −0.45), so the compression reads from the side;
- **membrane (no collider):** a finite rectangular compliant footprint, half extents 1.2 × 0.9 m (bed 2.4 × 1.8 m), rest height 0.2 m, maximum stroke 0.65 m along the frame's local +Y normal, declared as `CompliantSurfaceDescriptor` and compiled to `CompliantSurfaceDeclaration`.

The corner posts stand at the rail corners, outside the stroke volume, so a corner drop meets the bed and not a post.

### Mass, material, joints, ports, sensors
- Frame: static, no mass. Rim and back material: restitution 0, bounce threshold 0.1 m/s, friction 0.3, rolling resistance 0: the rigid rim and back absorb, and only the membrane stores energy.
- Membrane: a massless spring approximation, not cloth.
- Joints, ports, sensors, events: none. The part is passive; it needs no motor or trigger and reports no bounce.
- The membrane is one law driven by the declared surface data: an external force applied once per 480 Hz substep, not a joint or solver constraint row, with no element-, shape- or orientation-specific branch. Each body meets the bed at four support samples.

### Work and energy stores
- **Elastic store.** Σ 0.5·(k/4)·(dᵢ − oᵢ)² over a body's bearing samples, with k the `tension` and oᵢ each sample's offset (below).
- **Force law (four samples with offsets).** Each body meets the bed at its support points in the four diagonal directions −n ± ε·right ± ε·forward (ε = 1/64), found by the generic collider support query, so an axis-aligned box presents all four bottom corners, a flat face its extreme rim points and a sphere four points next to its lowest point. A sample *bears* the bed when it is `OnTop` (below), over the bed and below the rest plane, a purely geometric condition; it then carries a quarter of the bed: Fᵢ = max(0, (k/4)·(dᵢ − oᵢ) − (c/4)·vᵢ) along the frame normal, with dᵢ = clamp(rest − heightᵢ, 0, stroke), oᵢ its committed offset, vᵢ the normal velocity at the sample and c = 2·ζ·√(k·m). It never pulls.
- **Stroke.** dᵢ is clamped to the stroke, so beyond it the spring force saturates at the constant push (k/4)·(stroke − oᵢ), never more; the energy oracle counts the matching linear potential beyond the stroke. The frame's restitution-0 back plate sits at the stroke floor, so a load reaches the saturated region only by pressing into the plate.- **Contact state (per sample).** Each (surface, dynamic body, sample direction) has one committed typed state `Outside`/`OnTop`/`Under` and one f32 offset oᵢ in the world memory; Run and Reset zero them (every sample `Outside`, which classifies itself on the first update), and no save field carries them. A sample is *over the bed* when it lies within the half-extents plus the footprint edge e on both in-plane axes; dᵢ = clamp(rest − heightᵢ, 0, stroke). An entering sample is classified on its unclamped depth max(0, rest − heightᵢ), so a deep entry is `Under` whatever the stroke; the force and the stored offset use dᵢ. The state follows each substep's resulting pose, and the next substep's force reads it at that pose:

  | From | Condition | To, offset |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | any | not over the bed | `Outside`, oᵢ = 0 |
  | `Outside` | over, max(0, rest − heightᵢ) ≤ a | `OnTop`, oᵢ = dᵢ |
  | `Outside` | over, max(0, rest − heightᵢ) > a | `Under`, oᵢ = 0 |
  | `Under` | over, dᵢ = 0 (rose to the plane) | `OnTop`, oᵢ = 0 |
  | `Under` | over, dᵢ > 0 | `Under` |
  | `OnTop` | over | `OnTop`, oᵢ = min(oᵢ, dᵢ) |

  The committed deflection is the deepest dᵢ among `OnTop` over-bed samples, the visible indentation. The rule keeps three invariants:
  1. A sample that reaches the footprint on top bears the bed while it stays over the bed. On top means from above the plane, or crossing the edge no deeper than a.
  2. A sample that enters the footprint deeper than a is never pushed until it rises to the plane.
  3. The bed never returns more energy than it took: a sample starts bearing with its offset at its entry depth and the offset only shrinks, so re-entry of a tucked or rail-penetrating sample, a flipped-in sample and the crossing all cost no energy. Caveat: a support-direction jump between body features (at most about ε × the body's width, see below) can carry a sample's offset to a deeper point and so store up to that much unpaid compression.
- **Tolerances.**
  - **Arrival depth a, declared per surface (owner, 2 November 2026).** Each compliant surface declares its arrival depth (`CompliantSurfaceDescriptor.ArrivalDepth`, m). It travels with the other surface fields through the bundle payload, the compiled declaration, the ABI surface row (offset 12) and the compiled-scene wire record, and every boundary that refuses the other fields refuses a value that is not finite, positive and under half the stroke. A frame that declares a surface must leave a side opening (rest plane to the underside of whatever frames the footprint) of at least 2a, so a body entering under the frame is always `Under`.
    - **The Trampoline declares a = 0.10 m.** At tilt all four diagonal samples sit on a body's lowest end, so a hidden end becomes a sample only when the support flips to it. The deepest such flip-in measured over the shipped shapes and entries (straddles at 8.2 and 12 m/s, rail-first, tilted 20°, fast slides; the Weight from 0.25 to 8 kg) is 0.046 m at 300 N/m, (a 1.1 m Domino tipping in from a rail). a gives about twice that, and the frame's side opening (the rail underside is 0.2 m below the plane) is 2a. With offsets it admits no unpaid energy. Rail-top penetration does not size it: a load landing on a rail top at 12 m/s sinks 0.2–0.3 mm. a must be re-derived if the frame, the sample law or the catalogue's widest body changes.
  - **Footprint edge e = 1 cm (engine constant).** A flush-contact tolerance, independent of any frame's dimensions: it covers a load pressed flush against a frame face (its edge samples sit a fraction of a millimetre past the footprint). It is not widened, so a sample on a rail top beyond the footprint edge does not count as over the bed.
  - **Product ranges in shared admission (defect).** Shared admission bounds every compliant surface's tension to 120–1200 N/m and its damping ratio to 0.08–0.8 (`CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/PhysicsDeclarationValues.cs:89`–`:90`; `CuriousContraptions.Bundle/BundleValidator.cs:706`–`:707`). Those are this package's own parameter minima and maxima (`TrampolineElementDeclaration.cs:27`–`:28`), so the engine holds a package-sized limit and a second compliant part cannot declare other values without an engine edit. The descriptor also admits only one planar rectangular surface per element, on the static root, with its normal fixed to local +Y (`CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/MechanicalContractValues.cs:222`–`:234`). Owners: the one-surface-per-element slot belongs to `ENGINE-CAPABILITY-INSTANCES`; the product ranges in admission give way to `ENGINE-COMPLIANT-FRICTION`'s stability-bounded admission (coordinator decision 29).
- **Known costs and limits.**
  - **Cost (defect).** The contact-state pass samples every dynamic body for every surface each substep, and each sample scans the scene's whole collider list four times (`CuriousContraptions.Simulation/WorkshopPhysicsEngine.cs:1455`–`:1464`, `:1536`–`:1547`); the force pass repeats it for engaged bodies. Engagement rows are dense, one per surface and dynamic body (`engine/gpu/PhysicsWorldLayout.cs:41`–`:42`). The broadphase is not used. Owners: broadphase-bounded sampling, `ENGINE-SPATIAL-QUERIES`; the dense surface × body reservation, `ENGINE-CAPABILITY-INSTANCES`.
  - **Sag bias.** A load that never leaves the bed keeps the offsets it entered with: its resting sag lies in [m·g/k, m·g/k + max oᵢ]. A load that falls onto the open bed has every sample over the bed and `OnTop` from above the plane, so its offsets are 0 and its sag m·g/k; offsets arise only for samples entering below the plane (edge crossings, flip-ins), at most a. Bouncing loads reset on each re-entry above the plane.
  - **Support-direction identity.** A direction's support can jump between body features, by about ε × body width (about 1.7 cm for the 1.1 m Domino); the state inherits the direction. At tilt the samples collapse onto the lowest end, so a body whose lowest end is past the footprint edge is not borne even while most of it is over the bed (seen as a body leaving under a rail loses the bed when its lowest rim crosses the edge).
  - **Under-rail exit.** A body pressed below the rail underside next to a rail can slide out under the rail to the plate, with no energy gain, only if its vertical extent is under the 0.45 m opening: an upright Weight up to about 1 kg, a lying Weight up to about 0.6 kg and a lying Domino can; the Basketball (0.68 m) and the default 4 kg Weight cannot.
  - **No tangential friction (defect for play).** Compliant surfaces have no tangential friction or rolling resistance: the membrane pushes only along its normal, so a load on the bed slides freely along it until a rail stops it (a Weight thrown in by a 12 m/s straddle still drifts at 0.4 m/s after 8 s). The repair is bed friction through the general friction law, per bearing sample and energy-removing only, in `ENGINE-COMPLIANT-FRICTION`.
- **Clear region.** The Basketball (r 0.34 m) dropped from 7 m clears the rails when it lands with |x| ≤ HalfX − r = 0.86 m and |z| ≤ HalfZ − r = 0.56 m. Outside it the ball meets a rail's inner top edge as it sinks: the rigid, inelastic rail edge deflects it away from the rail across the bed with no energy gain, and it is never thrown sideways along the rail or pinned against it, and while over the frame its speed across the rails stays within half its arrival speed. Where it finally comes to rest is not part of the contract.
- **Committed read.** `CompliantSurfaceRead(Frame, Deflection)` carries each surface's deepest `OnTop` over-bed sample depth (0 at rest) on the response wire; the bed visual binds it.
- **Stability envelope.** The membrane is an explicit per-substep spring and damper on a body's bearing samples. Each sample carries stiffness k/4 and damping c/4, with c = 2ζ√(k·m) computed from the body's mass m. Let J be the bearing samples' constraint rows (normal n and lever r×n about the centre of mass) and M the body's mass matrix. Each mode of J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ, with eigenvalue λ, is stable while (k/4)·λ·h² + 2·(c/4)·λ·h < 4.
  - Equivalently, ω·h < 2(√(1 + ζ_eff²) − ζ_eff), with ω = √((k/4)·λ) and ζ_eff = ζ·√(m·λ/4). For n coincident samples at a point of effective mass m_eff = 1/(1/m + (r×n)·I⁻¹·(r×n)), λ = n/m_eff and ζ_eff = ζ·√(n·m/(4·m_eff)). Because the damper is sized from the body's mass, an off-centre mode is damped harder than ζ, and that tightens the bound.
  - Figures below are at k = 1200 N/m, ζ = 0.8 and h = 1/480 s.
  - A centred load bears on four near-coincident samples: λ = 4/m and ζ_eff = ζ. It is stable above about 5.6 g.
  - Off-centre rocking modes reach λ ≈ 11.4/m for a flat box with the Domino's proportions (corners at 0.55 m and 0.325 m, 0.125 m thick), stable above about 34 g. They reach λ = 12/m for a slender square rod (four end corners, length dominant), stable above about 37 g.
  - The Weight (0.25 kg minimum), the Domino (0.4 kg) and the Balloon (0.125 kg minimum) clear these bounds. The Tennis ball does not: its `mass` parameter admits 1/1024 kg, about 0.98 g (`elements/TennisBall/TennisBallElementDeclaration.cs:16`), below the 5.6 g bound derived above. Its behaviour on a stiff, heavily damped bed is unproven; residual row T-R3.
- **Binding.** Every dynamic body owned by another part is a potential load. A surface frame may be declared only once. The surface starts unloaded.

### Parameters

The parameters are declared by key in the package metadata and bound to the surface through `MechanicalValue.FromParameter`. The default tension is 300 N/m: the 1 kg Basketball dropped from the lesson's 7 m (8.2 m/s at the bed) peaks near 0.51 m of the 0.65 m stroke and stays on the membrane, where 180 N/m would bottom out on the back plate.

### Presentation and art
- **Bed.** `Visual/Visual_Bed` binds `PresentationDriver.CommittedSurfaceDeflection` on its local Y: flat at its 0.19 m rest height (top face on the rest plane) at rest and after Reset, and lowered by (0.65 − 0.001)/0.65 m per metre of committed deflection, so at full stroke its top face stays 1 mm above the back plate's.
- **Open requirement criterion: direction-aware sag.** The requirement asks that "direction-aware bounded sag follows committed deformation" (`docs/planning/requirements.md:848`). The delivered bed is one flat slab translated by the deepest deflection, so a second, shallower load visibly hovers above the slab. The criterion was deferred in a review loop of 10.5, not by the owner. It is not waived. Its repair, a per-sample committed deflection read and a height-field presentation binding, is owned by `ENGINE-COMPLIANT-FRICTION` (coordinator decision 29), within the engine programme (owner decision 3a).
- **Art.** Navy `#293954` back plate, cream `#fff8e9` rails, gold `#e8b764` posts and feet, cyan `#66b8c9` bed. Pick radius 1.8 m (the frame is 2.72 m wide). Icon: `elements/Trampoline/icon.svg`, a ball above a sagging bed, distinct from the Springboard's.
- **Catalogue.** Category Motion, description "A passive compliant membrane bed rebounds falling payloads upwards." Planned level `a_gentle_rebound` stocks one Trampoline.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Tension semantics.** Key `tension`, unit N/m (`ParameterUnit.NewtonPerMetre`): the bed's stiffness shared over four samples. Renaming would change saved constructions for no gameplay gain.
2. **Membrane model.** Resolved (Story 10.5 review loop 1): several samples per body. Each body meets the bed at four diagonal support samples carrying k/4 each, so flat payloads rest flat and boxes present all four corners.
3. **Bottoming out.** Resolved (Story 10.5 review loop 1; wording corrected in loop 5): the spring force saturates beyond the stroke at the constant push (k/4)·(stroke − oᵢ), and the frame's restitution-0 back plate at the stroke floor stops travel.
4. **Impact observation.** Not carried: the package declares no event row and no requirement or lesson reads a bounce count (D19).
5. **Placement assistance.** A placed Trampoline is static during Run; assistance may only move it in Build. A kinematic move during Run would be a prescribed launch, which the requirement forbids.
6. **f32 bounds.** No frozen list: each test states its bound from the quantity (energy never above the arrival energy, sag against m·g/k), under the [float rules](../../testing-guidance.md#float-comparison-and-effort-rules).
7. **Tether order.** Resolved by the 9 Oct 2026 reorder: rope Story 10.2 now precedes trampoline Story 10.5, so the tether criterion no longer has to move.
8. **Level number.** `a_gentle_rebound` is level 61 in `content/puzzles.json`.

**Verdict: Does not conform as delivered.** The package itself is declaration data and no engine code names it (searched `engine/`, `CuriousContraptions.Simulation`, `CuriousContraptions.Bundle` and the abstractions). 1. **Declaration data only.** One static body, thirteen scene colliders, one material, one `CompliantSurfaceDescriptor`, two parameters, one presentation binding. No bounce event, no launch, no script.
2. **Generic laws.** Rebound: the compliant-surface law (unilateral spring and damper per bearing sample, clamped at the stroke) with declared half-extents, rest height, stroke, arrival depth, tension and damping ratio. Rim, back and miss: ordinary contact with restitution 0. The per-sample `Outside`/`OnTop`/`Under` state is engine state of that law for any declared surface, a typed enum, not a state machine in the package. The footprint edge of 1 cm is an engine constant shared by every surface. The law is not yet general: its admission carries this product's ranges (first violation above).
3. **Three layers.** Colliders: plate, four rails, four posts, four feet (hidden proxies). The bed is a declared surface, not a collider. Visuals: the frame meshes and `Visual_Bed`. Animation: `Visual_Bed` local Y from `CommittedSurfaceDeflection`. The bed art does not push anything: the law does. The animation layer cannot draw the required direction-aware sag (third violation above). For light, the bed occludes as its declared sheet, a criterion of `ENGINE-OPTICAL` ([EL-176](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md) section 10, item 6); the bed art itself never occludes.
4. **One-way flow and typing.** Law → committed deflection read → presentation; nothing flows back. Typed parameters and `PresentationDriver`; f32 values.
5. **Flat-state counts.** 1 static body; 13 colliders (5 boxes, 8 cylinders), counted from `trampoline.tscn`; 0 joints, drives, nodes, stores, events, ports; 1 compliant surface; 2 parameters; 1 presentation binding. Engagement rows are sized at Run as surfaces × dynamic bodies × four samples, which is dense (second violation above).
6. **Performance fitness.** Thirteen static colliders is the largest static count in this batch; each backs a drawn frame part. The posts and feet are not coaxial, so they cannot be merged without redrawing the frame. The membrane law's per-substep cost is proportional to surfaces × dynamic bodies × colliders in the scene (second violation above); it is repaired in `ENGINE-SPATIAL-QUERIES` and `ENGINE-CAPABILITY-INSTANCES`, not left to the performance gate. 7. **Scenario tests.** `TrampolineTests` drives the production simulation with real packages; the lower-level state tests exercise the generic surface law, not a Trampoline path.
