## 3. Findings, most severe first

### Critical

**C-1. EL-025 does not tip.** `EL-025-tipping-bucket-water-clock.md:25,55`.
(a) The spec places contents "at their centroid" and its disqualifier (`:134`) says a body-origin rule means "the bucket would never tip". The repaired L12 b (`EL-001-finite-reservoir.md:37`) puts contents at the body's centre, translation only, as `CenteredInventory` does ("inventory adds translation only", `CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/BodyMassSourceDeclaration.cs:20`). With the shell's centre of mass 0.10 m behind the pivot, added mass there holds the bucket on its stop harder. Under the adopted convention the part cannot work.
(b) With true contents centroids it still does not tip. `:55` says the turn "runs away" once the contents pass 0.0625 m³. The shell's centre is 0.20 m below the pivot, so its restoring moment grows as 0.10·cos θ + 0.20·sin θ, faster than the liquid shifts. Net moment about the pivot (N·m, forward positive), liquid at the level relation of L4, hinge friction ignored:

| Volume held | 0° | 10° | 15° | 20° | 30° | 45° | 60° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0625 m³ | 0.00 | −0.20 | −0.29 | −0.37 | −0.50 | −1.08 | −1.67 |
| 0.08 m³ | +0.27 | +0.15 | +0.09 | −0.03 | −0.47 | −1.08 | −1.67 |
| 0.10 m³ (brim) | +0.59 | +0.34 | +0.17 | −0.03 | −0.47 | −1.08 | −1.67 |

The bucket leans to about 19°, stays brim-full (C(19°) = 0.078 m³) and weirs the inflow over its lip. It never reaches the 60° threshold, so `ActivationOut` never fires and rows C1, C3 and C4 fail. Moving the declared centre up does not rescue this box: at (−0.10, −0.05) the balance moves to about 43°; at (−0.10, 0) it passes 60° but the empty bucket no longer returns from the stop (+0.17 N·m at 100°).
Decision needed (coordinator, with EL-001's owner): (1) L12 b reads "contents add ρ·V at the centroid of the liquid region given by L4; no inertia is added", a general rule any tilting vessel needs, with a new `BodyMassSourceKind`; and (2) the part is re-dimensioned against a moment table like the one above. Recommended form: the twin-chamber see-saw the spec rejected at `:120`. Two stores on one rocker are bistable by geometry: the raised chamber fills until its moment passes the lowered one's, the rocker falls to its stop, the lowered chamber empties because its retained capacity at the stop angle is near zero, and each fall is one rising crossing for the threshold node. It needs no capability beyond a second store. Until both are settled the row "tips and discharges" has no working declaration.
(c) `:107` C5 uses C(45°) = 0.0375 m³. tan 45° = 1 is above H/w = 0.8, so L11's second branch applies: C·H/(2·w·tan θ) = 0.10 × 0.40 / (2 × 0.50 × 1) = **0.040 m³**. `:56` should give both branches.

**C-2. EL-029: cargo in the bilge displaces bilge liquid; V5 is false.** `EL-029-boat.md:58,109`. The leak calculation takes the bilge level as V_b / 0.59. A Basketball sitting in the hull is a dynamic body inside the bilge store's interior, so by L4 and L12 a its submerged part raises the bilge level and is buoyed. Solving level y = (V_b + V_d(y)) / 0.59 with the ball's cap volume V_d(y) = π·y²·(1.02 − y)/3 against the outside head (2 + 16·V_b)/11.52 − 0.05: the leak closes at y = 0.263 m, V_b = 0.100 m³, draught 0.313 m, freeboard 0.037 m. The leaky boat with a Basketball stays afloat. The spec's "total 4.83 kg, it sinks" assumes a bilge that fills to 0.177 m³.
Two convention gaps make even that result uncertain (section 7, E-2 and E-3): the buoyancy on the ball is not reacted on the boat, so as written the boat sheds the ball's weight as the bilge rises; and the ball's lower part is also inside the Lock chamber's interior and below its surface, so L12 a as written buoys it from the chamber through the hull floor (0.0144 m³ at V2's draught, a quarter of a kilogram).
Replacement once E-2 and E-3 are adopted: V5 uses cargo that does not float in the bilge. A 2.0 kg Weight is 24 kg/m³ (section 5) and its 0.26 m radius fits the 0.82 × 0.72 hull. Sound: draught 3.0 / 11.52 = 0.260 m, afloat. Leaky: the Weight's own displacement is counted, so the bilge level is V_b / (0.59 − 0.205) and the leak head is 0.210 − 1.21·V_b; the gunwale goes under at V_b = 0.0645 m³ (3 + 1.03 = 4.03 kg) after about 2.9 s, with the leak still running, and the boat sinks. V2 keeps the Basketball. `:58` is rewritten on these numbers.

**C-3. Nested interiors and the envelope's shielding are undefined.** `R-g:54` (LG7) and L8, L12 a. A bilge store, a bucket or a porous block sits inside another store's interior. Nothing says which interior a stream sample, a probe or an immersed mouth resolves to, or that the space inside a hull's envelope is not part of the surrounding store. It decides EL-029 V2, V4, V5, the rain line `:59`, EL-034 J1 and EL-014. Proposed text is E-2.

**C-4. EL-023: the rotor disc covers the outlet.** `EL-023-archimedes-screw.md:25,28`. The rotor is a solid Cylinder of radius 0.15 on the upper end face, the bore's own radius. The outlet is the lowest interior point of that end, normal +X, with aperture A₀ (contracted stream radius 0.077 m). Samples above the outlet point run into the disc (axial 1.00 to 1.06 m, radius 0.15), and samples below it start inside the Tube wall. Under L8 about half the stream is spilled, and the art shows liquid passing through a solid disc (visible = solid). A1 ("the tank gained what the basin lost") fails. Decision needed: where the liquid leaves. One consistent form: a spout, a static box 0.20 × 0.04 × 0.20 under the upper end with the outlet at its tip, clear of the tube and rotor by at least 0.08 m on every side of the stream; the pulley stays on the end face. The author recomputes `:28`, `:59` and the tank position: the tube's underside clears a Water tank's 1.15 m rim only for x ≥ 0.934 m from the part origin, and the tank's inner face is then at x ≥ 1.054 m, so the stream must cross 0.11 m of wall top; it does at 2.42 m/s and at the 1.57 m/s end-of-lift flow, and does not at a trickle.

### High

**H-1. EL-023 against the repaired L15 and owner decision 16 (coordinator decision).** `:26` ("With no input the edge is closed in both directions"; τ = min(20 W/|ω|, 4 N·m)), `:57` ("the edge stays closed, nothing rises and nothing drains back"), `:107` A4, and `R-g:53` LG6.
Decision: **the screw accepts the open edge and declares no one-way seat.** Both options keep element-023's row ("Wrong rotation or a dry intake cannot deliver the target volume"), so the choice is which is honest. A one-way seat from intake to outlet would do nothing: the intake is an Intake port (inward only), the outlet a free end (outward only, L8) and the fitting holds no volume, so nothing can run back with or without a seat. What the open edge allows is downhill conduction when the supply surface stands above the outlet datum. In any upright placement over an open vessel that cannot occur: the tube must cross the rim, and the outlet lies beyond and above that crossing. It can occur only if a player tilts the part, and then it behaves as a pipe, which is the same rule the pump follows.
Replacements. `:26`: "…input is the rotor's revolute coordinate in a declared positive sense. The energy taken is the hydraulic work ÷ 0.5: resisting torque = (ρ·g·Q·h_p ÷ 0.5) ÷ |ω|, never above min(20 W / |ω|, 4 N·m) (L15). Undriven, or driven the other way, the work target adds no head and the edge is open." `:57`: "A negative coordinate rate is not input: no head is added. The outlet datum is above the supply surface, so nothing passes (L7 crest rule)." `:54`: at 5 rad/s the shaft absorbs 8.97 / 0.5 = 17.9 W (3.59 N·m), not the rated 20 W. `:60`: the lift takes 156.96 × 1.25 × 0.4375 / 0.5 = **172 J**, not 232 J. A4 becomes `WaterScrewTests.AnUndrivenScrewAddsNoHead` with the boundary "the part tilted so its outlet is 0.2 m below the basin surface: liquid runs through it undriven". `R-g:53`: "…Motion the other way absorbs nothing and adds no head; the edge is open, as any ungranted work target is." The delivered precedent for a signed coordinate input is `CoordinateAirflowInput(…, Sense)` (`CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/AirflowValues.cs:38`), used by the Fan with −1 (`elements/Fan/FanElementDeclaration.cs:55`); cite it in the capability row.
One wording gap in L7 affects this part and EL-022: the crest rule counts only "the highest total head among the wet stores", so read literally no work target can lift above its supply. E-1 fixes it.

**H-2. EL-168 and EL-171: a one-way release follows the peak of an under-damped press.** `EL-168-siphon-priming-bulb.md:25,55,107`; `EL-171-squeeze-pad.md:25,55,108,112`; `EL-024-primed-siphon.md:95` (P3). Both stores release what exceeds C(x) and take nothing back, so the delivered volume is set by the largest travel reached. With 100 N/m and 10 N·s/m a 1.5 kg Weight on a 0.2 kg press has damping ratio 10 / (2·√(100 × 1.7)) = 0.38 and overshoots by about 27%: peak travel 0.174 m against the static 0.137 m. The bulb then delivers 0.02 × 0.174/0.25 = 0.0139 m³, above the Siphon's 0.0125 m³: the "insufficient" case primes, and Y2, P3 and the requirement outcome "Insufficient priming leaves the siphon broken" fail. The pad releases 0.0217 m³ where Z1 expects 0.0171 within 0.004, and Z5's 0.0015 m³ becomes 0.0061. Replacement in both `:25` lines and `R-g:153`: "damping 40 N·s/m" (ratio 1.53 with the 1.5 kg Weight, 0.98 with a Bowling ball: no overshoot), and "set on the cap at rest" in each row. Both tables then stand. Row Z4 proves little as written (a fall in potential energy is the descent again); add the boundary "a 0.1 kg load (2.94 N against the 3 N preload) does not move the plate and releases nothing".

### Medium

**M-1. EL-166: static angles asserted for a dropped Weight; L5 cannot shut the lever.** `EL-166-mechanically-actuated-tap.md:58-59,110,114`. The stopping angles are where a slowly loaded lever stalls.
