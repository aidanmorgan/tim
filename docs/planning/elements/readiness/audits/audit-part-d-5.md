## Verdict: Fail

The seating arithmetic is sound: every seating and port move, bar five descriptive misstatements, recomputes (table below). The Fail is for three things inside or under the frozen intent: (F1) the Wound spring socket drop cannot discriminate, because the sleeve's end ring stops the Basketball 7.2 mm below the socket, inside the 17 mm bound; (F2) the M2 axle drop defines its stop as the lowest 120 Hz sample, which can sit 20 mm above the contact, so the 5 mm bound is unsatisfiable, and its 17 mm lower bound is the Basketball's, not a quantity for a 0.0625 m ball; (F3) five wrong statements inside `<intent-contract>`. With F1-F3 and R1-R7 applied the contract is "Scoped Pass (ready, blocked on the programme)". A re-audit can be limited to the diff.

### F2. M2: the stop is read from samples, and its bounds are borrowed from the Basketball
- Where: intent row M2 (line 50); line 171; line 176.
- Defect 1: "the lowest centre height reached before the ball first rises" is a 120 Hz sample. The impact speed from 0.3 m is sqrt(2 g 0.3) = 2.43 m/s, 20.2 mm per commit, so the lowest sample can lie up to 20 mm above the contact and the "5 mm above" bound fails on most phases. `AssertDropOnto` meets this for sphere apexes by reading the contact height as where the free-flight arc through the last falling read meets the arc through the first rising read (`Meet`, `VisibleSolidColliderTests.cs:623`, doc at 505-519); a sphere on a rod is an apex. The prior audit's suggested wording (adopted by the author) omitted that construction.
- Defect 2: "17 mm below" is `Penetration` = 5 % of the Basketball's 0.34 m radius (`:412`); for a 0.0625 m ball the envelope rule ("temporary visual penetration <= 5 % of the smaller body's extent", `docs/gpu-f32-physics.md:368`) gives 3.1 mm. A 17 mm bound is 27 % of the ball's radius and would admit an axle collider 17 mm too small. (The collider-against-art row at 2e-6 m covers size; the drop must still not be loose by an unrelated constant.)
- Defect 3: `AssertDropOnto`, `BallRadius`, `BallAt` and `Meet` are private to `VisibleSolidColliderTests` and bound to the Basketball; `HingeFacePrintTests` cannot call them, and the Never clause forbids changing shared harness code. The contract does not say what the new class uses.
- Fix (intent row M2): "... | Its first fall ends on the axle: the contact height, read as `VisibleSolidColliderTests.AssertDropOnto` reads a sphere apex (the height where the free-flight arc through the last falling commit meets the arc through the first rising commit), lies between 5.4 mm below and 5 mm above the axle's top plus the ball's radius (0.055 + 0.0625 = 0.1175 m above the part origin); it may then roll off either side | No error expected |".
- Fix (line 171, replace the sentence "The axle drop uses the drop theory's bounds, 17 mm below ... a ball passing through the axle would drop to the base, more than 0.4 m below, so the bound discriminates."): "The drops read the contact height from the two free-flight arcs, as `AssertDropOnto` does for a sphere apex, because the 120 Hz commits are 20 mm apart at the 2.43 m/s impact. The lower bound is the envelope penetration, 5 % of the ball's 0.0625 m radius (3.1 mm), plus the arcs' meeting bias e/(1+e) v h = 0.85/1.85 x 2.43 / 480 = 2.3 mm: 5.4 mm; the upper bound is `AssertDropOnto`'s 5 mm. A ball passing through the axle would drop to the base, more than 0.4 m below. `HingeFacePrintTests` carries its own drop helper with this construction and a comment giving the derivation, with the Tennis ball package at radius 1/16 m (`WorkshopInput.Generic` with a `radius` parameter); `VisibleSolidColliderTests`' private helper is not changed."
- Fix (line 176, M2 row, Control/Boundary columns): unchanged except the bounds words above; the boundary geometry (ball x 0.0425 to 0.1675, 10 mm from the coil's face at 0.0325) and the control (x 0.4, z -0.2: 12.5 mm clear of the right block face 0.475 at ball x max 0.4625; lands on the base top -0.4 at centre -0.3375) recompute.
- Classification: test (intent row M2 changes with it).
