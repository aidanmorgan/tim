### Behaviour and numbers

- **Laws.** Light: the diverging-segment distance law sampled at nine declared points with occlusion and conserved shares, and the whole power of a collimated segment that ends on the face. Conversion: the absorbed-energy credit into the finite store `cell` (coordinator decision 21). Availability: the signal network's threshold rule with release ratio 0.9. Power: the supply network's reservation from an ordinary source on a store, through a signal-state contact. The part adds no law.
- **Reading at 3 m.** The Flashlight radiates 90 W into 0.2141 sr, so I = 420.4 W/sr and, beyond 1 m, E = I × facing ÷ d², d measured from its lens face (inside 1 m it is flat, I ÷ 1 m² for a source in front of the face, optical 1; no sample here is inside 1 m). Centre sample: 420.4 / 9 = 46.7 W/m². A corner sample is √(0.35² + 0.45²) = 0.570 m off-axis: d² = 9.325, facing 3 / 3.054 = 0.982, so 420.4 / 9.325 × 0.982 = 44.3. The side samples give 45.8 (d² = 9.1225, facing 0.993) and 45.2 (d² = 9.2025, facing 0.989). Ē = (46.7 + 2 × 45.8 + 2 × 45.2 + 4 × 44.3) / 9 = 405.9 / 9 = 45.1 W/m². All nine lie inside the cone, whose radius at 3 m is 3 × tan 15° = 0.80 m.
- **Power.** Absorbed P_abs = A × Ē = 1.8 × 45.1 = 81.2 W, below the 90 W the torch radiates, and the panel is the only receiver in the cone, so no share is scaled. Converted 0.2 × 81.2 = 16.2 W, that is 0.2 × 81.2 W × dt = 0.135 J credited per default 1/120 s tick (0.271 J per 1/60 s tick), inside the 0.30 J store. Nearer than about 2.84 m the uncapped product would pass 90 W (at 2.13 m, where the cone first covers all nine samples, Ē would be 86.7 W/m² and the product 156 W); the conserved-share rule holds the reading at 90 W, so P_abs never exceeds 90 W and the credit never exceeds 18 W × dt (0.15 J a default tick, 0.30 J a 60 Hz tick). The torch pays 100 W for that light, so the pair never gains energy, and output can never exceed 0.2 × absorbed energy because the source draws only what was credited.
- **Availability at P_abs ≥ 31.5 W** (Ē ≥ 17.5 W/m², the legacy level of one unit, 420.4 / 24 = 17.5; the four-mark meter then means "supply is available"), released below 0.9 × 31.5 = 28.35 W (Ē 15.75 W/m², shown as 15.8). On the axis 420.4 / d² = 17.5 at d = 4.9 m; with the sample geometry Ē is 18.4 W/m² (33.1 W) at 4.75 m, 5 % above the level, and 16.6 W/m² (29.9 W) at 5.0 m, 5 % below it and inside the release band. Between there and the 8 m range the marks show partial light and a dark panel supplies nothing: 6.70 W/m² at 7.9 m lights one mark. Supply does not latch beyond the release band: when P_abs falls below 28.35 W, `available` turns off at the start of the next tick and the contact opens. At the availability level the converted power is 0.2 × 31.5 = 6.3 W.
- **A Laser on the cells.** A collimated segment that ends on the face is absorbed whole (Story 13.7 names "Flashlight or Laser"). One amber Laser delivers 21 W: Ē = 21 / 1.8 = 11.7 W/m², two marks and no supply (21 W < 31.5 W). Two amber Lasers deliver 42 W (23.3 W/m², 33 % above the level): four marks and 0.2 × 42 = 8.4 W converted.
- **Allocation.** Loads share the source's per-tick allowance, min(store balance, 18 W × dt) (energy per tick = power limit × dt, coordinator decision 30, `ENGINE-SUPPLY-DEMAND`; the delivered allowance uses `ElectricalSupply.PhaseSeconds = 1/120`, `engine/gpu/ElectricalSupply.cs:9`, with `CuriousContraptions.Simulation/WorkshopPhysicsEngine.Supply.cs:79`–`:82`, the same at the default cadence), in proportion to their requests (`ElectricalSupply.Reserve`, `engine/gpu/ElectricalSupply.cs:11`).
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The default Motor can ask up to 20 N·m × 6 rad/s = 120 W; at 16.2 W an unloaded rotor still turns (once spun up it pays only bearing loss, 0.05 N·m × 6 rad/s = 0.3 W, which even the 6.3 W at the availability level covers). A loaded one is limited by the over-allowance rule of coordinator decision 27 (23 November 2026; `ENGINE-SUPPLY-DEMAND`): a drive whose request in a tick exceeds its source's allowance is limited to it, its torque scaled so its work that tick equals the energy granted (today drive supply is binary, so a 120 W Motor would run from a 16 W panel). A default 4 kg Weight on the 0.3 m rim tie of `MotorTests.cs:619` loads the rotor with 4 × 9.81 × 0.3 = 11.8 N·m while the tie is level: the drive keeps its full 20 N·m only below 16.2 ÷ 20 = 0.81 rad/s and then turns at about 16.2 ÷ 11.8 = 1.37 rad/s (faster as the tie turns and the load's arm shortens), so the load rises at no more than 16.2 W ÷ (4 kg × 9.81 m/s²) = 0.41 m/s. Rows P5 and P6 prove it. The 0.22 m slab is a wall to every ball.
- **Ambient light.** Scene lighting is not an emitter. With no declared emitter active the reading is exactly zero.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. For this part: the energy booked on the panel's collider record in tick n is credited to `cell` at the end of tick n's solve (step 3), before tick n's thermal step and commit, and `available` reads tick n's reading at the start of tick n + 1, so the source draws tick n's light during tick n + 1's substeps; light arriving and a motor responding are one tick apart, and no row asserts it.

### Engine conformance (22 November 2026)

Checked against the owner's engine-conformance instruction ([element-free engine design](../../../_bmad/custom/element-free-engine-design.md); AGENTS.md, Tri-Graph architecture). The single transfer form used here is coordinator decision 21's, which is also the only form the optical conventions admit (coordinator decision 25).
