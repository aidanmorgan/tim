## 5. Acceptance

### Behaviour and numbers

All values are starting values (D1) under the [optical conventions](#optical-conventions).

- **Emission law.** While the `enable` level is on and the supply grants energy, the emitter sends one collimated segment of (10, 0, 0) W × (energy granted over the tick ÷ energy demanded). Ten watts is one legacy unit: four times the default 2.5 W detector threshold, so the beam still lights a detector after a 50/50 splitter (5 W), after two mirrors (10 × 0.95² = 9.03 W) and after a combiner and a mirror (10 × 0.9 × 0.95 = 8.55 W).
- **Supply.** Demand 12 W = 12 W × dt per tick, 0.1 J at the default dt of 1/120 s. The delivered Battery allows at most `power_limit` × dt per tick (coordinator decision 30), 1 J at its 120 W default and the default dt (`elements/Battery/BatteryElementDeclaration.cs:44`–`:45`), so ten such lasers run from one default Battery, and its 3,600 J last 300 s for one. Radiant 10 W ≤ demand 12 W: the 2 W difference is the declared loss, ledgered to the housing box (the first internal occluder, convention 2), and never becomes light. At the Battery's minimum `power_limit` of 10 W the grant is 10 ÷ 12 and the beam carries 8.33 W.
- **Range.** 16 m from the lens point; nothing is lit beyond it.
- **Tick order.** Routing and the latch are resolved at the start of tick n; energy is granted inside tick n's substeps; the optical solve runs after the last substep of tick n on that grant and on tick n's final poses; the reading commits with tick n and acts on a detector's contact from tick n + 1. A tick in which supply is open grants nothing, so that tick has no beam; the latch is untouched.

1. **Channels and units.** Light has three channels, a closed set: Red, Green, Blue. Power is watts per channel, f32: P = (R, G, B). One legacy "game optical power" unit is 10 W. A diverging emitter has radiant intensity I = P ÷ Ω in W/sr, Ω = 2π(1 − cos θ) for half-angle θ. Irradiance at a point beyond 1 m is E = I × facing ÷ d² in W/m², where facing = max(0, −d̂·n) for a flat aperture; inside 1 m it is E = I ÷ 1 m² for a source on the front side of the aperture (facing 1) and 0 for a source behind it, so a reading is flat inside 1 m and never rises as a source moves away. This is the one near-field rule for light and radiation **(coordinator decisions 21 and 26)**. Every rule below is written over a declared channel set, so a solve with more channels (radiation) adds no rule.
2. **Emitters.** A declared node: frame body (static or dynamic), origin, axis, half-angle (0 = collimated, up to 180°), range, radiant power per channel, demand (at least the sum of the channels), internal occluders, and an optional enable that names a signal level. Its payer is any finite conversion: a supply consumer target, a declared store, or a fuel burn. Over a tick it emits radiant × (energy granted over the tick's substeps ÷ energy demanded), so light never exceeds energy paid. Granted energy not emitted is ledgered as a loss to a declared collider of the emitter, by default its first internal occluder (the housing), and becomes heat only as thermal T3 moves it. An emitter whose origin lies inside another collider emits nothing.
3. **Beam representation.** The solve produces segments: origin, direction, half-angle, power per channel, remaining range and depth. A collimated segment is a zero-width ray and carries its whole power to what it ends on. A diverging segment is terminal: it lights receiver sample points directly and starts no further segment. Redirecting responses (Reflect, Relay, spread) apply to collimated segments only, except that a Relay inlet declared as a receiver aperture takes its conserved share of diverging segments (convention 8). Between its origin and a sample a diverging segment is multiplied by each Transmit surface's τ and by each Split's τ, the Split's ρ share being absorbed and booked on the Split's collider record (a Split is applied once per crossing, at its entry face); it is blocked by everything else.

4. **Tick order: the one combined order for every domain (coordinator 16, coordinator 26).** This item is the single statement of the tick order. The liquid (L16), pneumatic (F5), thermal (T8) and radiation (radiation 11) conventions cite it and do not restate it. **dt** is the committed tick's duration: 1/120 s by default, 1/60 or 1/240 s by the Run's simulation cadence (`SimulationCadence`, `CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/CadenceValues.cs:54`–`:56`); a tick has 8, 4 or 2 substeps of 1/480 s (`CuriousContraptions.Simulation/WorkshopPhysicsEngine.cs:297`). Every per-tick sum, credit and accumulator in every convention uses dt and the tick's substeps, never a constant rate. For tick n:
   1. **Tick start.** The signal network resolves routing and memory nodes (latch Set and Reset, threshold levels) from tick n − 1's committed observations (D7), and the tick's supply is prepared (`WorkshopPhysicsEngine.cs:294`). Then, still before the substeps, **the acoustic phase** (Story 14.0; engine stories index C21): it serves the emission requests and qualified contacts committed in tick n − 1 and the observation excitations over tick n − 1's committed observations, advances occurrences in flight, and computes each receiver's band level against tick n − 1's committed poses; the levels commit with the other observations at step 5. So a contact committed in tick n − 1 excites in tick n, and the acoustic phase reads no grant of tick n, which is made later (step 2(c)): what a paid emitter reads of its supply is the supply route's powered state as resolved at this step from tick n − 1's commits.
   2. **Each substep, in this order.** (a) The gas step (pneumatic F5). (b) The liquid step (liquid L16), which moves each transfer's heat with its volume (thermal T2) and in which each gas–liquid edge reads the gas pressure step (a) has just committed and debits that gas store (liquid L15). (c) Supply grants (`WorkshopPhysicsEngine.cs:1011`): a liquid or gas work target therefore spends the grant of the previous substep (at the first substep, tick n − 1's last), and a grant not spent when Reset is pressed is lost. (d) The mechanical solve with its trial replays; friction and drive work accrue from the accepted solve only. (e) Commit of constraints and coordinate travel. The gas and liquid steps run before the solve and are not repeated by a trial replay (the replays sit inside `SolveBoundedSubstep` and `SolveContinuousSubstep`, `WorkshopPhysicsEngine.Motors.cs:30`–`:60`).
   3. **After the last substep, the one optical and radiation solve.** It reads this tick's final collider poses and liquid levels, the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps (grants are made inside the substeps, `WorkshopPhysicsEngine.cs:1011`, `WorkshopPhysicsEngine.Supply.cs:72`–`:107`), and the signal levels resolved at step 1. It books absorbed energy once, on each absorbing collider's record (items 2 and 7; radiation 10), and ends by applying each declared store credit (item 9) from that record.
   4. **The thermal step** (thermal T8): source credits (supply grants, friction and drive work summed over the substeps, inventories), then each thermal face's absorptance share of what remains on its collider's absorbed record (thermal T3), then the link exchange (thermal T9) and phase rows on the post-exchange values.
   5. **Sensors and commit.** Liquid levels, receiver readings and strengths, accumulators, temperatures and pressures are evaluated; segments, readings, poses and every domain's state commit together.
   6. **Tick n + 1.** Committed observations enter the signal network at its step 1 and the electrical solve reads them (D7), as contact loads do (`CuriousContraptions.Simulation/WorkshopGpuDevice.cs:246`–`:249`). Mass a phase row moved in tick n's thermal step enters its liquid or gas store at the tick boundary and moves from tick n + 1's substeps.

   So light closes a supplied contact one tick later; a gate's control switches its carrier one tick later; a tick with no grant has no beam. Readings are sums over emitters, so emitter order cannot change them. `docs/gpu-f32-physics.md` draws optics in Phase 2, before the substeps; DOC-ENGINE-STATUS records the difference. A grant at any cadence is energy per tick = power limit × dt, stated by ENGINE-SUPPLY-DEMAND (coordinator decision 30); the delivered supply phase is still 1/120 s at every cadence (`ElectricalSupply.PhaseSeconds`, `engine/gpu/ElectricalSupply.cs:9`), which that story replaces.
5. **The spatial query.** Every candidate comes from ENGINE-SPATIAL-QUERIES' segment cast; the optical solve never scans colliders. The cast returns, in order along the segment, every crossing of every participating collider: entry point, exit point, normal, collider and feature. It is exact for all seven admitted shapes (Sphere, Box, Plane, Cylinder, Tube, Torus, Frustum; `CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/PhysicsDeclarationValues.cs:12`), including hollow ones crossed twice and tori on moving bodies. A response is applied once per crossing of a collider; a continuing segment starts at the exit point; **range is consumed by segment length only; a crossing's chord consumes none:** a segment that continues, is transmitted or is split at a crossing keeps the remaining range its parent had at the entry point (the parent's range less its length from origin to entry), whether it starts at the entry point (a reflection) or at the exit point (a transmission), so a 16 m beam that enters a 0.03 m pane 6 m from its lens leaves 10 m on each branch (CAT-008 R7); a reflected or relayed segment ignores the face that produced it, and a segment that leaves a Relay outlet ignores the collider carrying that outlet at its origin. **Own body, one rule for light and radiation (coordinator 26).** A shadow cast from a source to a receiver's sample ends at the receiver's own aperture face (for a Uniform receiver, radiation 7, at the sample point itself); every crossing before that end acts by its declared response or attenuation, the receiver's own colliders included, and nothing beyond it is read. For light a sample is therefore lit only when no Absorb crossing precedes its aperture face; for radiation each preceding crossing attenuates it by its transmittance below. The same cast serves airflow line of sight (replacing `AirflowLineClear`), acoustics, and radiation, whose attenuation by chord length uses entry and exit: per channel, transmittance through a crossing is the declared surface τ × exp(−μ × chord), with μ = 0 for light.
6. **What occludes.** Every collider with collision participation enabled, static or dynamic, at this tick's final pose. Art never occludes and physics never reads art. Each collider has one declared response; the default is declared per channel: τ = 0 (**Absorb**) for light channels and τ = 1 for radiation channels (radiation 4). Delivered packages whose clear shells must declare Transmit (1, 1, 1) on those colliders, as criteria of ENGINE-OPTICAL: Pipe, Pipe bend 45, Pipe bend 90, Funnel (shell and stubs), Powered gate (sleeve), Cannon (jacket) and Wound spring (sleeve). A compliant surface occludes as its declared sheet. Visible art with no collider lets light through as it lets bodies through. The Fan's blades are decorative, turned by a presentation binding (owner decision of 21 November 2026; coordinator decision 25), and pass light by design; the Fan's ring and post and the delivered rope are existing visible-solid defects, repaired by their owners (repair part A; ENGINE-CONNECTION-MATERIALS, owner decision 1), with no optical exception. A solid connection strand occludes like any collider. An emitter skips its declared internal occluders on its first segment only.
7. **Surface responses, declared on a collider face or a disc aperture of it.** Absorb: the segment ends; the absorbed power is booked once, on the absorber's record, and becomes heat only as thermal T3 moves it. Reflect ρ per channel, front face only: d′ = d − 2(d·n)n, P′ = ρ ∘ P. Transmit τ per channel, either face: P′ = τ ∘ P, with an optional spread half-angle that makes the continuing segment diverging. Split: Reflect and Transmit on one aperture with ρ + τ ≤ 1 per channel. Relay: the segment ends on an inlet aperture and a new segment leaves each declared outlet (position, direction, half-angle, τ, channel mask), its range reduced by the declared internal path; an outlet may instead declare its own range (TH-06), and its position and direction may be declared relative to the inlet hit point and the incoming direction, as an offset and a rotation (RAD-14); an optional enable names a signal level; an outlet may be on another placed part joined by an Optical-domain connection. A declaration whose outputs can exceed its input in any channel (ρ + τ > 1, or the sum over a Relay's outlets of τ × mask > 1) is refused at bundle time. Refraction is declared as its result (a spread, or Relay outlets), never solved from indices.
8. **Receivers.** A declared node on a collider face (static or dynamic frame): aperture (disc or rectangle), area A, sample points with weights summing to 1, a sensed channel set, and a strength rule over it: Minimum over a declared required set, or WeightedSum with declared weights (the mean of three channels is weights of 1/3). A purity fraction, where declared, is the required set's share of the sensed set's total; strength is zero when purity fails. The reading is the sum of collimated segments ending on the aperture plus each diverging segment's share. **Shares are conserved (coordinator 16):** for each diverging segment the solve sums the uncapped shares A × Σ wᵢEᵢ of all receivers, a Relay inlet declared as a receiver aperture among them; if the sum exceeds the segment's power, every share is scaled by power ÷ sum, so received never exceeds emitted. A receiver whose aperture is on an Absorb face books what it receives (the collimated power ending on it plus its conserved diverging shares) once, on that collider's record; its reading observes that same power and books nothing again, and no reading credits anything by itself (coordinator 26). One law serves every kind of light (owner decision 9): a detector answers a laser or a Flashlight alike, and no receiver has a "collimated only" attribute.
9. **Coupling (coordinator 16).** A receiver commits a continuous reading and a strength. Its level is a signal node, "threshold over a committed scalar, with a release ratio" (on at strength ≥ threshold, off below ratio × threshold), owned by ENGINE-SIGNAL-NODES. That is the only control coupling: supply contacts use the delivered signal-state control (`CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/SignalContactControl.cs:12`); a Relay or emitter enable names a signal level; gate logic is a boolean node of the signal network. No contact, relay or logic reads a receiver directly. Absorbed energy reaches a supply in one form only, the store credit of coordinator decision 21 (coordinator decision 25): a declared fraction, at most 1, of the energy booked on the receiver's absorbing collider in the tick moves from that record to one declared finite store that an ordinary supply source draws from, at the end of the solve (item 4, step 3), before the thermal step reads what remains (thermal T3); a credit that does not fit the store stays on the record. A part that gates the source's availability does so with a threshold signal node (Solar panel CAT-059: 0.2 × absorbed into a 0.30 J store, one 60 Hz tick of its largest credit as coordinator decision 32 sizes every credited store, availability by a threshold node; light-charge receiver EL-154: 0.5 × absorbed into its parameter-bound store, no threshold). The store's values are the part's own declaration. **The credited store** (a supply source whose finite store has a declared capacity, power limit, initial charge and enable, as constants or bound to parameters, and gains energy during a Run only by a credit) **is one row of ENGINE-THERMAL**, which lands before ENGINE-OPTICAL (owner decision 18; coordinator decision 26, 22 November 2026; Batch O G20). The receiver credit is ENGINE-OPTICAL's row; a thermal converter's credit is ENGINE-THERMAL's. There is no allowance form.
10. **Limits.** A path ends after 16 interactions (requirement [current-cat-041](../requirements.md#current-cat-041)); an emitter produces at most 128 segments (requirement [current-cat-008](../requirements.md#current-cat-008)); a segment ends when its remaining range is at most 1e-4 m or its total power is below 0.001 W. At the segment limit the solve keeps segments breadth-first by depth, then by emitter and surface order. ENGINE-OPTICAL confirms or resets these once.
11. **Work per tick (coordinator 26).** With N emitters and segment limit L = 128: at most N × L segment casts, plus one shadow cast per sample point of each candidate receiver of each diverging segment, each costing what the spatial query's segment cast costs. A diverging segment's candidate receivers come from the spatial query's region query over its cone and range (ENGINE-SPATIAL-QUERIES); no other receiver is tested, so no term is proportional to the whole scene. A Transmit spread creates diverging segments at run time, so the number of diverging segments is not a compiled count and is bounded only by L.
12. **Presentation.** Beams, cones, lens glows, lamps and meters are art driven by bindings or by the shared beam renderer reading committed segments and readings. The build-mode aim preview is the same solve with emitters assumed on, committing no reading; ENGINE-OPTICAL states where the preview's collider set comes from in Build, where nothing is compiled, without a second solve.
