### Behaviour and numbers

- **Laws.** Gas at ambient pressure: m_gas = p·V ÷ (R·T). Lift (coordinator decision 28): F = V·ρ_amb·g·(1 − T_amb ÷ T_env), which at ambient pressure is exactly (ρ_amb·V − m_gas)·g, the displaced air's weight less the envelope air's, capped at 0.432 N. Net vertical force on the balloon and what rests on it = F − (m_dry + m_cargo)·g, less the drag 8 × m_dry × v on the balloon. Heat by links (T3). No speed is prescribed.
- **Constants.** ρ_amb·V = 1.2259 × 0.9048 = 1.1092 kg; V·ρ·g = 10.881 N; p·V ÷ R = 101 325 × 0.9048 ÷ 287 = 319.4 kg·K. The rise ΔT that gives a lift F is ΔT = 288 × f ÷ (1 − f) with f = F ÷ 10.881. The cap 0.432 N is reached at f = 0.0397, ΔT = 11.9 K.
- **Cost of lift.** Heating the open envelope from T₁ to T₂ costs c_p·(p·V ÷ R)·ln(T₂ ÷ T₁) = 321 040 × ln(T₂ ÷ T₁) J, about 1115 J/K near 288 K. Lifting m kilograms costs about c_p·288·m = 289 kJ per kilogram whatever the envelope's size, which is why the structure is 27 g and the crate 12 g.
- **Lift-off, empty.** F must exceed 0.027 × 9.81 = 0.2649 N: f = 0.02434, T = 295.19 K, a rise of 7.19 K, costing 321 040 × ln(295.19 ÷ 288) = 7.92 kJ. The bound acceleration is then exactly 9.81 m/s².
- **On a Heating plate.** The pan rests on the disc (32 W/K); 120 W less the disc's loss reaches the gas through 4 W/K: about 106 − 2.5·ΔT W for a gas rise ΔT, so the final rise is 42.4 K with a time constant 1115 ÷ 2.5 = 446 s. By hand lift-off at t = −446 × ln(1 − 7.19 ÷ 42.4) = 82.9 s; tick by tick (disc 15.25 J/K, pan 2 J/K, gas 321 040 ÷ T J/K) the gas passes 7.19 K at 88.5 s, having drawn 10.6 kJ from the Battery: a default Battery (3600 J, 30 s; the gas rises under 3 K) cannot do it; one with `capacity` 14 400 J can, and it empties at 120 s with the gas 9.6 K up.
- **It then leaves its heat.** Off the disc the contact link is gone; the pan's stored heat adds under 0.1 K and the gas then cools at 2 W/K: the lift falls back below the weight within seconds and the balloon settles. It hovers within a few centimetres of the disc and does not climb away.
- **Excess load.** With a Tennis ball (`radius` 0.15 m, `mass` 0.35 kg) on the deck the load is (0.027 + 0.35) × 9.81 = 3.70 N, above the 0.432 N cap: never, at any temperature. The lift law alone also holds it down: 3.70 N needs f = 3.70 ÷ 10.881 = 0.340, a rise of 288 × 0.340 ÷ 0.660 = 148 K, against the 9.6 K the plate and the 16.2 K the bowl bring.
- **Lifting one cargo crate (owner decision 23).** The layout is TH-02's "held over it": the balloon's pan rests on two Walls laid flat across the rims of a lit Fire bowl, its underside at 0.30 m inside the flame box (0.175 to 0.675 m) and its 0.40 × 0.40 m bounds covering the whole 0.16 m² box (G = 4.0 W/K). The pan runs near 345 to 350 K and passes about 200 W to the gas (the second audit's tick iteration), so the gas rises as ΔT(t) ≈ 100 × (1 − e^(−t ÷ 557)) K, 16.2 K when the bed burns out at 98 s. With its 0.012 kg crate at its seat pose (the deck's centre), lift-off needs F = 0.039 × 9.81 = 0.3826 N: f = 0.03516, T = 298.49 K, a rise of 10.49 K, costing 321 040 × ln(298.49 ÷ 288) = 11.5 kJ, at t = −557 × ln(1 − 10.49 ÷ 100) = 61.6 s, 36 s before burn-out. The bound acceleration is then 0.3826 ÷ 0.027 = 14.2 m/s², 11% under the cap. With the bowl unlit no heat reaches the gas, which stays at 288 K, so the bound lift is 10.881 × (1 − 288 ÷ 288) = 0 N and the 0.3826 N load stays down by the lift law itself (A9's control, coordinator decision 32). The crate lifts for any delivery above about 200 × 10.49 ÷ 16.2 = 130 W; TH-02's figures for a body over the bed lie between 164 W and 214 W. On the Heating plate the gas reaches only 9.6 to 10 K (120 W, 14 400 J), just short of the 10.49 K one crate needs; no row depends on that margin.
- **Flight with the crate.** After lift-off the pan stays inside the flame box until its underside passes 0.675 m (0.375 m of climb) and keeps gaining about 0.16 K/s, which adds 0.0347 N/K × 0.16 = 0.0057 N/s of net force; the drag holds the speed to the net force ÷ (8 × 0.027) = net force ÷ 0.216 N·s/m, so the climb speed grows at about 0.026 m/s per second and the pan leaves the box after about 5 s at about 0.14 m/s. Out of the box the gas cools at 2 W/K (about 0.024 K/s), the net force falls by about 0.0008 N/s, and the balloon slows, stops after about 40 s and settles. The speed can never pass (0.432 − 0.3826) ÷ 0.216 = 0.23 m/s with one crate (the cap) or (0.432 − 0.2649) ÷ 0.216 = 0.77 m/s empty, both inside the 1.2 m/s bound.
- **Why drag 8 s⁻¹.** The body's inertia is its 27 g dry mass, so a linear drag sized for the Balloon (0.4 s⁻¹) would let a 0.05 N surplus drive it at 4.6 m/s. 8 s⁻¹ makes 0.216 N·s/m, the order of the real aerodynamic drag of a 1.2 m sphere at walking pace (½ × 1.2259 × 0.47 × 1.131 × 0.5² = 0.08 N at 0.5 m/s, 0.16 N·s/m): the balloon moves slowly, as a hot-air balloon does. It is stable in the engine's substep (8 ÷ 480 = 0.017).
- **The crate's own displaced air.** Decision 28 makes buoyancy in air a declared lift, and the crate declares none, as the balloon's struts and deck declare none. Its displaced air would be ρ_amb·V·g = 1.2259 × 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.81 = 0.0072 N, 6% of its own weight and 2% of the one-crate lift; leaving it out makes the lift row slightly harder.
- **Balance.** The ambient load adds to the body's gravity acceleration (`CuriousContraptions.Simulation/WorkshopPhysicsEngine.Gas.cs:108-111`), so it acts at the centre of mass and gives no righting moment. The crate at its seat pose, the deck's centre, adds no moment; a crate off-centre would tip the deck, and no row uses one.
- **Vacuum.** No ambient density: the compiled lift is zero at any temperature.
- **Tick order.** Heat enters the gas in the thermal step of tick N (T8); the store's committed temperature sets the lift, and the store's vented mass changes, for tick N + 1's substeps.
- **What a gap of 0.005 m means.** At lift-off the net lift is only what the last fraction of a kelvin adds, so on the plate the pan rises slowly and leaves its heat (above): the hover height is not a reliable 0.02 m. Rows assert that the contact ends and the gap passes 0.005 m at least once (audit, TH-25 A1).
- **Run length these rows need: 230 s** (A3 observes 120 s after a lift-off of up to 1.15 × 88.5 = 102 s; A1's stall guard is the smaller of 3 × 82.9 and 82.9 + 60 s, 143 s; A9's is the smaller of 3 × 61.6 and 61.6 + 60 s, 122 s; A6 observes 60 s after a lift-off of up to 1.15 × 61.6 = 71 s; ENGINE-RUN-LENGTH, owner decisions 13 and 17; default 30 s, maximum 300 s).
- **Tolerances** (T12): temperature 2 K; mass 0.5%; times 15%.

## 6. Open questions

Resolved:

1. **Game scale.** D5 as restated in the thermal conventions.
2. **Power requirement.** Decided now: keep SI air and make the structure 27 g with a declared 2 W/K fabric loss, so an empty balloon lifts for 7.9 kJ, inside a 14 400 J Battery on the plate. A burner of its own, a special gas scale or an authored hot start would each be a second rule.
3. **Map wording.** The requirement row's open envelope governs (D15).
4. **Gas time constant.** Accepted: 557 s to cool, which is what lets a warmed balloon fly for a while.
5. **Heat path.** Decided now: heat enters through the metal pan under the deck (contact or a flame's region link) and reaches the air by a wetted link. A region link straight to the envelope would ignore the deck that sits in the way.
6. **Tether.** No rope port (D19): no thermal lesson ties it; a rope would also wait for solid strands.
7. **One buoyancy law (coordinator decision 28, 23 November 2026).** Buoyancy in air is the declared ambient lift; this part's lift is bound to its committed envelope temperature by the decision's law, and the delivered Balloon keeps its constant declared lift. There is no displaced-density law beside it.
8. **Cargo (audit owner item 7; owner decision 23, 23 November 2026).** No delivered part is light enough (the Tennis ball is 0.35 kg). The companion package `balloon_cargo`, a 0.012 kg crate, is sized so it lifts over a lit Fire bowl about 36 s before burn-out. The crate rides on the deck by contact, with no strand. It is declared not placeable (owner decision 24b, 23 November 2026): it has no palette entry and comes with each balloon at the balloon's seat pose on the deck, and is removed with it (coordinator decision 33).
9. **Mass, cap and drag under decision 28 (decided 23 November 2026 under the standing delegation, by game intent).** Decision 28's lift already subtracts the envelope air's weight, so the air is not a mass source of the body; the body's inertia is then its dry mass alone, and the engine applies the lift as an acceleration on that mass. A declared acceleration lies inside 16 m/s² (`CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/WorldAccelerationDeclaration.cs:25`), so the bound lift is clamped there, 16 × m_dry. With the earlier 15 g structure the cap would be 0.24 N, below the 0.383 N the crate needs, so the dry mass rises to 27 g: the cap, 0.432 N, sits 11% above the crate's lift-off load of 0.3826 N, so the lift law, not the cap, decides when the crate lifts (A9), and the empty balloon still lifts on the plate (88.5 s of a 120 s Battery). The cap is a declared property of this envelope (its useful payload), not a numerical guard, and no row passes only because it binds: A2's Tennis ball needs a 148 K rise by the law alone, and A6's 1.2 m/s bound holds without the cap (the uncapped surplus at burn-out, 10.881 × (1 − 288 ÷ 304.2) − 0.3826 = 0.197 N, gives at most 0.197 ÷ 0.216 = 0.91 m/s). A heavier dry mass would need more rise on the plate than a 14 400 J Battery brings (0.036 kg needs 9.7 K; the plate brings 9.6 K). The 8 s⁻¹ drag replaces the Balloon's 0.4 s⁻¹ because the inertia no longer includes 1.1 kg of air (section 5).
10. **Two-crate control (coordinator decision 32, 23 November 2026).** Decision 31 asked that the two-crate control fail by the lift law alone at the envelope temperature the bowl brings, never only because the 0.432 N cap binds. No dry mass and crate mass met that together with A1: at the bowl's peak (ΔT 16.2 K; F from 0.511 N to 0.647 N across T12's 2 K band) a two-crate row held down by the law alone needs a dry mass above 0.647 ÷ 16 = 0.040 kg, while the plate's 9.6 K lifts at most 0.351 ÷ 9.81 = 0.0358 kg. So the two-crate row is deleted, and A9's control is the same one-crate layout with the bowl unlit: no heat reaches the gas, the bound lift is 10.881 × (1 − 288 ÷ 288) = 0 N, and the balloon stays down by the lift law itself.
11. **The crate's pose in rows (coordinator decision 33).** Each balloon comes with its crate at the seat pose. Rows that need an empty deck (A1 to A5 and A7) author the crate off it, as EL-084 and EL-088 author another fish pose; A10 proves the seat pose, Reset, save and removal with the balloon.

Needs the owner: none. Needs the coordinator: none. Coordinator decision 34 settles the seated companion: the crate stays on its seat (the player cannot move it off), but the player may delete it alone in Build and restore it from the balloon, and a level may place the balloon without it; Reset restores the authored state either way. The plate lesson (levels 79b and 81, row A1) needs an empty deck, because the plate brings the gas to 9.6 to 10 K and the crate needs 10.49 K: the level places the balloon without its crate, or the player deletes it.
