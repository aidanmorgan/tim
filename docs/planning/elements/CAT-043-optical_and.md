## Declaration

No delivered part has an optical declaration. The nearest delivered shape for a five-member logic family is the electrical gates: one static scene body and one `LogicGateOperation` value per package (`elements/ElectricalNand/ElectricalNandElementDeclaration.cs:9`, `:31`, `:35`). There the operation sits on a supply logic node (`SupplyLogicDescriptor`, `CuriousContraptions.Abstractions/SupplyContractValues.cs:41`); here it sits on a boolean signal node, because the optical gate has no socket and no supply node: its inputs and output are light, and its controls couple to the carrier only through the signal network.

### Behaviour and numbers

- **Laws.** Optical transport and absorption at the two controls; the receiver rule; the signal network's threshold rule with its release ratio for `a` and `b` and its boolean rule for `open`; the Relay law for the carrier, its enable naming `open`. The gate creates no light: output = 0.9 × the carrier that entered, or nothing; the other 10 % and all control light are absorbed and ledgered (not heat until ENGINE-THERMAL, decision K-10).
- **Carrier.** Amber (10, 7.8, 3.2) W × 0.9 = (9, 7.02, 2.88) W, mean 18.9 / 3 = 6.3 W. Two gates in series: × 0.81 = (8.1, 6.32, 2.59) W.
- **Control levels reachable with real parts.** Amber mean 21 / 3 = 7.0 W: high. One split 3.5 W: high. Two splits 1.75 W: low. One gate's outlet as the next gate's control: 6.3 W, high; a control fed through n Relays turns a low control high while 7.0 × 0.9ⁿ ≥ 2.5 W, that is n ≤ 9 (0.9⁹ = 0.387 → 2.71 W; 0.9¹⁰ = 0.349 → 2.44 W).
- **Hold band (row G5).** *Proposed* feed, in place of the A Laser: a Laser on its own Battery; 1.2 m to a Beam splitter turned 45° (its reflected branch ends on a Wall); 2.0 m to a Mirror turned 45° (the two bases, each reaching about 0.9 m along the beam, stay clear), which sends the transmitted branch along A's axis; 1.2 m to the First input of Beam combiner C1; C1, C2 and C3 in line on A's axis, First input to outlet (internal path 1.40 m each, CAT-006), each First input 1.0 m beyond the previous outlet; C3's outlet 2.0 m in front of A. The Relays are Beam combiners, always enabled, so the feed depends on no other gate's state. Path 1.2 + 2.0 + 1.2 + 3 × 1.40 + 2 × 1.0 + 2.0 = 12.6 m, inside the 16 m range (3.4 m left). Power 7.0 × 0.5 × 0.95 × 0.9³ = 3.5 × 0.729 × 0.95 = 2.424 W mean ((3.46, 2.70, 1.11) W). It lies 3.0 % below 2.5 W and 7.7 % above 2.25 W: it holds a high control and does not raise a low one; its ± 2 % band tops out at 2.47 W, under 2.5 W. The second Laser on A, on its own Battery, stands 3 m from A's centre, 45° off A's axis on the −Z side: where C3 begins (2.0 m from A) that beam is 2.0 m off the axis, clear of C3's 0.9 m half-base. With C3 removed (C2's outlet then 4.4 m from A): 3.5 × 0.81 × 0.95 = 2.693 W, 7.7 % above 2.5 W: it raises a low control. Without the Mirror's loss the hold feed would be 3.5 × 0.729 = 2.552 W, 2.1 % above 2.5 W, and would raise a low control.
- **Cone light.** One receiver law serves every kind of light (owner decision 9). A Flashlight cone is a terminal diverging segment: it starts no further segment, so the carrier inlet relays none of it and the gate only occludes it. A control reads its share A × Σ wᵢEᵢ over its centre sample, with shares conserved (conventions item 8; coordinator decision 16). The Flashlight emits (30, 30, 30) W over a 15° half-angle: Ω = 2π(1 − cos 15°) = 0.2141 sr, 140.1 W/sr per channel. With its lens face 3 m from A on A's axis, facing it, the irradiance at A's sample is 140.1 ÷ 3² = 15.57 W/m² per channel and A reads 0.581 m² × 15.57 W/m² = 9.05 W per channel (9.04 W from unrounded values), mean 9.05 W: high. At 7 m, still inside the torch's 8 m range: 140.1 ÷ 49 × 0.581 = 1.66 W, low (34 % below 2.5 W). The mean reaches 2.5 W at d = √(0.581 × 140.1 ÷ 2.5) = 5.71 m. **Geometry of row G13.** The cone's radius at 3 m is 3 × tan 15° = 0.80 m, wider than the 0.70 m half-face, so light spills past the −X face's edges, but it lights neither control B nor the carrier inlet. The lens lies in the planes y = 0 and z = 0, below the +Y face plane (y 0.70) and short of the +Z face plane (z 0.70), so every ray from it meets those planes travelling in +y or +z, onto their back sides: facing = max(0, −d̂·n) = 0 for both front-only apertures. Their centre samples are also shadowed: the line from the lens (−3.70, 0, 0) to B's sample (0, 0.70, 0) enters the cube through the −X face at y = 0.70 × 3.0 ÷ 3.70 = 0.57 m, inside the face and outside A's 0.43 m disc, so the cube absorbs it; a ray to the carrier inlet's centre (0, 0, 0.70) is the same case about z, and the inlet would relay no cone light in any case. The receiver 3 m beyond the outlet lies on the same axis behind the cube, which shadows its centre sample. So A is the only receiver the cone reaches, its share over three channels (3 × 9.05 W) is below the 90 W emitted, and no scaling applies. At 7 m the same planes and the same shadow (y = 0.70 × 7.0 ÷ 7.70 = 0.64 m) apply, and the outlet receiver is beyond the torch's range.
- **Range.** The declared internal path from the carrier face centre (0, 0, 0.70) to the outlet (0.70, 0, 0) is √(0.70² + 0.70²) = 0.99 m. A 16 m beam that enters after 6 m leaves with 16 − 6 − 0.99 = 9.01 m.
- **Interaction budget (row G7).** A path ends after its sixteenth interaction ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions) item 10; two facing Mirrors give 17 segments, `CAT-041-mirror.md` row R8), and the relay is one interaction of the carrier's path. So a carrier that reaches the inlet after 15 interactions is relayed as its 16th and exits; one that reaches it after 16 ends on the inlet and is absorbed there. *Proposed* fixture: two Mirrors facing each other, front caps 0.55 m apart (each base reaches 0.27 m in front of its cap, so the bases clear by 0.01 m), the second shifted 0.125 m along the corridor. The carrier Laser, its lens 0.70 m behind the second Mirror's cap plane (its base front 0.04 m behind that Mirror's base), sends the beam past that Mirror's 0.775 m backing at a step of 0.10 m per crossing, atan(0.10 ÷ 0.55) = 10.3° from the normal. Hits on the first Mirror run from −0.5875 to +0.6125 m about its centre and on the second from −0.6125 to +0.5875 m about its centre, each at least 0.0375 m inside the 0.65 m reflecting disc; the entering ray clears the second backing by 0.0375 m, and after 14 reflections the leaving ray passes the first backing at 0.8125 m, clear by 0.0375 m, in its entry direction. (An odd count cannot leave this corridor: entry and exit would both pass the same backing, 16 steps apart, which needs 16 × step > 1.55 m while 14 steps must fit the 1.30 m disc.) It then passes one Beam combiner, First input to outlet (15 interactions), or two in line 0.45 m apart (16), and reaches the carrier face 0.45 m beyond the last outlet; the first First input is 1.05 m behind the first Mirror's cap plane, so a combiner base turned 10.3° clears that Mirror's base, which reaches 0.63 m behind its cap. Range: lens to first hit (0.70 + 0.55) ÷ cos 10.3° = 1.27 m; 13 legs of √(0.55² + 0.10²) = 0.559 m, 7.27 m; last hit to the first combiner (0.55 + 1.05) ÷ cos 10.3° = 1.63 m; one combiner: inlet at 1.27 + 7.27 + 1.63 + 1.40 + 0.45 = 12.01 m, outlet at 13.00 m, a receiver 1.0 m beyond at 14.00 m; two combiners: inlet at 12.01 + 1.40 + 0.45 = 13.86 m with 2.14 m of range left, so only the interaction limit stops it. Power: 0.95¹⁴ × 0.9 = 0.439, carrier (4.39, 3.42, 1.40) W, leaving as (3.95, 3.08, 1.26) W; with two combiners the arriving (3.95, 3.08, 1.26) W is far above the 0.001 W cut-off.
- **Finite discs.** r 0.43 m on 1.4 m faces: a ray 0.40 m off-centre is taken by the aperture; one 0.46 m off-centre meets the cube and is absorbed.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** No socket and no electrical draw: a Battery is needed only by the Lasers. The 1.4 m cube is a wall to every ball.
- **Tick order and the next-tick decision.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. No optical solve is delivered today. Control strengths commit with tick n; the threshold nodes `a` and `b` and the boolean node `open` take them at the start of tick n + 1 in one feed-forward pass, and the optical solve at the end of tick n + 1 reads `open` for the relay's enable (1/120 s later at the default cadence). That is the requirement's "next-tick decision", a required feature; row G3 asserts it. A chain of k gates responds in k ticks.
