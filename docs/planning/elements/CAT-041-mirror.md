### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** The optical transport law carries a collimated segment to the disc's front cap; the Reflect response returns r = d − 2(d·n)n with each channel multiplied by its declared retention; the 5 % not reflected is absorbed by the mirror and ledgered (not heat until ENGINE-THERMAL, decision K-10). The part adds no law.
- **Cone light.** A Flashlight cone is a terminal diverging segment: redirecting responses apply to collimated segments only, and a diverging segment is blocked by every surface that does not declare Transmit ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions), item 3). This part therefore only occludes a cone and reflects none of it.
- **A 45° mirror turns a beam 90°.** Beam direction d = (1, 0, 0); the mirror turned +45° about +Z (right-handed), so its front normal (−1, 0, 0) becomes n = (−0.7071, −0.7071, 0). d·n = −0.7071. r = (1, 0, 0) − 2 × (−0.7071) × (−0.7071, −0.7071, 0) = (1, 0, 0) + (−1, −1, 0) = (0, −1, 0): straight down, at 90° to the incoming beam.
- **Where it lands.** With the mirror's origin at (0, 0, 0) and the beam along y = 0, the cap plane is (p − 0.18 n)·n = 0, so the hit is at x = −0.18 / 0.7071 = −0.255 m; it lies 0.18 m from the cap centre (−0.127, −0.127, 0), well inside 0.65 m. The reflected beam descends along x = −0.255.
- **Retention.** Amber (10, 7.8, 3.2) W × 0.95 = (9.5, 7.41, 3.04) W, broadband mean 19.95 / 3 = 6.65 W: a default receiver (on at 2.5 W, which is 0.25 legacy units; off below 2.25 W) lights. Two mirrors: 0.95² = 0.9025, giving (9.025, 7.04, 2.89) W. Sixteen mirrors: 0.95¹⁶ = 0.440, mean 7.0 × 0.440 = 3.08 W, still above 2.5 W: mirrors alone never starve an amber beam; range and the interaction cap end it.
- **One range budget.** The Laser's 16 m is shared by all segments. A mirror 6 m from the lens leaves 10 m for the reflected leg: a receiver 9.5 m down that leg is reached (15.5 m in all); one at 10.5 m is not (16.5 m).
- **Sixteen interactions.** A path ends after its sixteenth interaction, so two mirrors facing each other yield at most 17 segments. *Proposed fixture* (horizontal corridor at disc-centre height; lateral positions measured along the corridor): Mirror A square to the corridor, cap plane y = 0, centre at lateral 0.56 m. Mirror B facing it, cap 0.6 m away, centre at lateral 0.90 m, turned 2.15° about the vertical so that each reflection on it reduces the beam's angle from A's normal by 4.3°. Each mirror's base reaches 0.45 − 0.18 = 0.27 m in front of its cap, so with caps 0.6 m apart the bases are 0.06 m apart (0.03 m at B's turned corner, 0.825 × sin 2.15° = 0.031 m nearer). The beam enters past B's entry-side edge, crossing B's cap plane at lateral 0 (0.125 m clear of B's backing edge at 0.90 − 0.775 = 0.125 m) at 17.2° from A's normal. Traced exactly, its walk along the corridor slows, turns and comes back: the nine hits on A lie at lateral 0.186, 0.519, 0.751, 0.887, 0.932, 0.887, 0.751, 0.519 and 0.186 m (at most 0.373 m from A's centre, 0.277 m inside the 0.65 m aperture); the eight hits on B lie from 0.032 m on the far side to 0.523 m on the entry side of B's centre (0.127 m inside its aperture); after eight reflections on B the angle is 17.2 − 8 × 4.3 = −17.2°. The seventeenth segment ends on A's glass 0.373 m from its centre, so only the interaction limit prevents a seventeenth reflection. Path length from B's cap plane 10.5 m for all seventeen segments (9.85 m for the first sixteen); with the Laser's lens 3 m back along the entry line, 13.5 m in all, inside the 16 m range.
- **Physical controls.** The backing, the base and the disc's side absorb, as every collider does by default. A ray 0.67 m off-centre meets the cap's printed band between the aperture edge (0.65 m) and the disc edge (0.69 m) and is absorbed; a ray 0.73 m off-centre misses the disc, meets the backing inside its edge (0.775 m) and is absorbed. A beam met square-on returns along its own line and ends on the Laser's housing.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The 0.26 m backing is a wall to every ball and a ball rebounds from the disc's face; a ball or Wall on either leg occludes the beam.
- **Tick order.** A mirror holds no state. One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. The mirror reflects at its pose at the end of the tick's substeps. No optical phase is delivered today.

**Tolerances (the one optical set; coordinator decision 21).** Power readings ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.95 factor is 5 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Reflected direction 0.5°: a beam aimed at the centre of a 0.55 m target misses at the 16 m range beyond atan(0.55 ÷ 16) = 1.97°, and 0.5° is 0.14 m at 16 m, a quarter of that radius, so a law that misses the target at range fails. Segment end points and path length ± 0.02 m: under 5 % of the smallest aperture radius (0.43 m), so a path that would clip or miss an aperture fails. Exact: the segment count (a declared limit), authored Reset values and the absence of any segment or reading after Reset (intentional zero). Positions placed either side of an edge or the range (0.60 and 0.67 m about 0.65 m; 15.5 and 16.5 m about 16 m) are designed cases, not tolerances; those rows assert state only.
