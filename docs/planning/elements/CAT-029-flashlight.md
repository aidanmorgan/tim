### Behaviour and numbers

Distances to the torch are measured from its lens face, the emitter origin (x 0.625 in the torch frame, on the collar's front face).

- **Laws.** Impact: the contact law's pre-response approach speed against `trigger_threshold`, on the button body. Latch: signal memory. Light: the emitter law and the diverging-segment law of the conventions, with occlusion and conserved shares. Payment: the finite store ledger or the supply reservation law, by the `supply` choice.
- **Cone.** Half-angle 15° and range 8 m ([requirement row](../requirements.md#current-cat-029)). Radiant power (30, 30, 30) W = 90 W over the solid angle Ω = 2π(1 − cos 15°) = 2π × 0.0340742 = 0.2141 sr, so the radiant intensity is I = 90 / 0.2141 = 420.4 W/sr (140.1 W/sr per channel). Irradiance is E = I × facing / d² beyond 1 m and E = I ÷ 1 m² inside 1 m for a sample in front of the lens (facing 1; zero behind it), so a reading is flat inside 1 m ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions), item 1): on the axis 420.4 W/m² at and inside 1 m, 420.4 / 9 = 46.7 W/m² at 3 m, 420.4 / 64 = 6.57 W/m² at the 8 m range and zero beyond. The cone's radius at distance d is d × tan 15° = 0.268 d: 0.80 m at 3 m, 2.14 m at 8 m. The legacy intensity of 24 units becomes these watts (decision K-1): one legacy irradiance unit is 420.4 / 24 = 17.5 W/m².
- **Energy.** Demand 100 W, 100 W × dt = 0.833 J per tick at the default 1/120 s tick; 90 W is radiated and the other 10 W is the declared loss. Self-contained: the store lasts 14 400 / 100 = 144 s, then the torch is dark until Reset. Electrical: the default Battery allows 120 W, 1 J per tick at the default tick (`elements/Battery/BatteryElementDeclaration.cs:44`), so one torch is granted in full and a 3600 J Battery lasts 3600 / 100 = 36 s. Emitted power = radiant power × (granted ÷ demanded): a Battery with `power_limit` 60 W grants 60 / 100 = 60 %, and the torch emits (18, 18, 18) W, 0.6 × 46.7 = 28.0 W/m² on the axis at 3 m. Two torches on one default Battery ask 200 W and the delivered reservation law shares the 120 W allowance in proportion (`ElectricalSupply.Reserve`, `engine/gpu/ElectricalSupply.cs:11`; probe row R7), so each is granted 60 % and emits (18, 18, 18) W. A torch and a Laser (25 W) on one default Battery ask 125 W and each runs at 120 / 125 = 96 %. Reason for a store at all: the Solar panel turns this light into paid electrical work, so unpaid light would create energy. Reason for 14 400 J: it is the delivered Fan's store, and 144 s outlasts every lesson's stall guard by a wide margin.
- **Bound on what receivers can take.** A receiver's reading from this cone is its share A × Σ wᵢEᵢ; when the shares of one diverging segment summed over all receivers exceed its power, every share is scaled by power ÷ sum (conventions item 8; coordinator decision 16), so the receivers in the cone together never absorb more than the 90 W emitted. One receiver law serves all light (owner decision 9). The Solar panel (nine samples, face 1.8 m²) with its face 3 m from the lens face reads a mean of 46.7 × (8.687 / 9) = 46.7 × 0.9652 = 45.1 W/m² (centre 46.7; the four corner samples, 0.57 m off-axis, 46.7 × 0.948 = 44.3), so it absorbs 1.8 × 45.1 = 81 W, below 90 W, and nothing is scaled. Nearer than about 2.84 m the unscaled product 1.8 m² × the sample mean would exceed 90 W (at 2.13 m, where the cone first covers all nine samples, the mean is 86.7 W/m² and the product 156 W); alone in the cone, the panel's share is then scaled to 90 W. A broadband detector (disc area π × 0.55² = 0.950 m², one centre sample) on the axis with its disc 3 m from the lens face reads 0.950 × 46.7 = 44.4 W in total, 14.8 W per channel.
- **Two receivers share one cone (row F15).** Two broadband detectors (CAT-038: plate 1.4 m wide, base 1.6 m along z, one centre sample on a 0.950 m² disc), both square to the axis and staggered in depth so neither shades the other: A with its disc 2.3 m from the lens face and 0.55 m to +z of the axis, B with its disc 3.2 m from the lens face and 0.80 m to −z. (Side by side with touching plates, 1.4 m apart, does not place: the 1.6 m bases would overlap by 0.2 m.) Each sample lies inside the cone (A: 0.55 m < 2.3 × tan 15° = 0.616 m, 13.4° off the axis; B: 0.80 m < 3.2 × tan 15° = 0.857 m, 14.0°). A: d² = 2.3² + 0.55² = 5.5925 m², facing = 2.3 ÷ 2.365 = 0.9726, E = 420.4 × 0.9726 ÷ 5.5925 = 73.1 W/m², unscaled share 0.950 × 73.1 = 69.5 W. B: d² = 3.2² + 0.80² = 10.88 m², facing = 3.2 ÷ 3.298 = 0.9701, E = 420.4 × 0.9701 ÷ 10.88 = 37.5 W/m², unscaled share 35.6 W. The shares sum to 105.1 W, more than the 90 W emitted, so each is scaled by 90 ÷ 105.1 = 0.857: A reads 59.5 W and B 30.5 W, together 90 W. Alone, either detector's share is below 90 W and is not scaled. Placement (each detector's body runs from 0.295 m in front of its disc to 0.555 m behind it, and ±0.80 m in z about its centre): A spans 2.005 to 2.855 m from the lens face and z −0.25 to 1.35 m; B spans 2.905 to 3.755 m and z −1.60 to 0.00 m, so the bodies are 0.05 m apart in depth. The line from the lens to B's sample runs at z = −0.25 × x, −0.50 m where it passes A's front, 0.25 m clear of A's near edge. The 14 % between unscaled and scaled readings is the created energy the row excludes.
- **What the cone does to other optics.** A diverging segment is terminal (decision K-3): it lights receiver sample points directly and starts no further segment. Redirecting responses (Reflect, Relay, spread) apply to collimated segments only; a cone is multiplied by each Transmit surface's τ and each Split's τ (the Split's ρ share absorbed and ledgered) between the lens and a sample and blocked by everything else ([optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions), item 3). So a colour filter passes its own channel of the cone, a Beam splitter's pane passes half, and a mirror, a combiner's cube or a gate's cube only casts a shadow. A detector in the cone responds when its declared rule is met, and this torch is the white source (decision K-9).
- **Latching.** The contact node is bound to body 1, so only a hit on the button at or above `trigger_threshold` sets the latch; a hit on the barrel, collar or base does nothing. The spring, with its rest position 0.02 m above the upper stop, presses the button onto that stop with 60 × 0.02 = 1.2 N against its 0.05 × 9.81 = 0.49 N weight, so the button sits on its stop in every roll of the torch (upright is the worst case; owner decision 6's rule for a sprung part, which ENGINE-JOINT-KINDS delivers). A ball dropped 0.5 m arrives at √(2 × 9.81 × 0.5) = 3.1 m/s, well above the 0.8 m/s default; a ball resting on the button has no approach speed and does nothing. A pulse on `ActivationIn` sets the same latch.
- **Supply loss (Electrical).** A tick with no grant over its substeps has no segment. `enable` is signal memory and is untouched: when supply returns the cone returns with no new trigger (the delivered parallel: `SwitchElectricalTests.SupplyLossAndRestorationDoNotEraseOrReplayImpactMemory`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/SwitchElectricalTests.cs:72`, cited as in CAT-036).
- **Fit with delivered parts.** Triggers: a falling ball, or any Pulse output (Impact switch, Delay box as in `delayed_solar`, System clock). Supply in Electrical mode: a Battery or any switched `Supply`. The barrel is 0.6 m across and the whole part 1.2 m long: a solid obstacle, and too large for the pipe bore, 1.3 m across, to matter.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. For this part: a latch set during tick n is resolved at the start of tick n + 1, so the torch lights from that tick's solve; a Solar panel's supply follows its reading one tick later. No optical solve is delivered today.

Reason: the body binding is a declared value of the general contact node, so it needs no region test and no per-part callback (the legacy coordinate test of harvest fact 5 stays uncarried); DESIGN.md names a "pressing top button"; and a button that is its own moving body meets visible = solid without art moved over a fixed collider. 5. **Supply — decided (K-11):** a `supply` Choice of Self-contained (a fixed 14 400 J store, the delivered Fan's) or Electrical (`PowerIn`, demand 100 W).
