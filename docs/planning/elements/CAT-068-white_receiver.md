### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** Optical transport delivers channel power to the aperture; the receiver rule turns the reading into a committed strength; the threshold signal node turns the strength into the level `matched`; the supply network's contact law passes whatever supply reaches `PowerIn` to `Supply` while `matched` is on. The part adds no law of its own.
- **Amber alone.** The amber laser emits (10, 7.8, 3.2) W: every channel is present, strength = min = 3.2 W ≥ 2.5 W (0.25 legacy units), matched, 28 % above the default. This is intended: amber carries all three channels (decision D18), and one source that carries all three satisfies the rule as well as three sources do; the Flashlight, the white source (decision K-9), is delivered before this slice (Story 13.1) and satisfies it too (row R11). After one mirror blue is 3.2 × 0.95 = 3.04 W, lit, 22 % above. After one split it is 1.6 W, dark, 36 % below the threshold and 29 % below the 2.25 W release level.
- **Three lasers.** A red, a green and a blue laser on the target read (10, 10, 10) W: strength 10 W, matched. Merged by a Beam combiner they arrive as (9, 9, 9) W: matched.
- **Three filtered beams through a combiner.** Red-, green- and blue-filtered amber merged by a Beam combiner arrive as (9, 7.02, 2.88) W: strength 2.88 W, 15 % above the default, matched. With `threshold` 2.7 W it is matched (2.88 W is 7 % above); with 3.1 W it is not, from dark (2.88 W is 7 % below, inside the hold band 2.79 W to 3.1 W). Three amber lasers ask 3 × 25 = 75 W of the default Battery's 120 W.
- **Any channel absent.** Red and green lasers only, (10, 10, 0) W: B = 0, dark, although the mean is 6.67 W and a broadband receiver at the same threshold lights. The same holds for (10, 0, 10) W and (0, 10, 10) W, and for any single colour.
- **Per-channel boundary.** With three lasers and one of them through one splitter, that channel is 5 W: lit. Through three splitters it is 1.25 W: dark, 44 % below the 2.25 W release level, although the other two are four times the threshold. Each channel in turn. (Two splits give exactly 2.5 W, so no row uses them.)
- **Set rule.** The declared purity fraction 0.9 compares the required channels with all sensed channels; here they are the same, so the ratio is always 100 %. Unequal light is therefore accepted: three extra red beams give (40, 10, 10) W, still matched. That is the requirement's "not exact spectral matching". The six lasers ask 6 × 12 = 72 W.
- **Forcing a three-beam mix.** A level that must not be solved by one amber laser sets `threshold` above 3.2 W. At 5 W amber fails (min 3.2 W, 36 % below the threshold and 29 % below its 4.5 W release level), three lasers pass (10 W each) and three lasers through a combiner pass (9 W each).
- **Threshold range 0.5 to 20 W.** At 0.5 W an amber beam still lights it after two splits (blue 0.8 W) and not after three (0.4 W, below the 0.45 W release level too). At 20 W, three lasers of each colour light it (30 W each) and one of each does not (10 W, below the 18 W release level). Nine colour lasers ask 9 × 12 = 108 W of the default Battery's 120 W, so each emits at full power.
- **Release ratio 0.9** (decision K-4 of [readiness/batch-k.md](readiness/batch-k.md): a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact). A matched receiver stays matched until its strength falls below 0.9 × `threshold`; a dark one needs the full `threshold`. With `threshold` 3.36 W the band runs from 0.9 × 3.36 = 3.02 W to 3.36 W, and one amber beam (weakest channel 3.2 W) lies inside it: 4.8 % below the threshold and 5.8 % above release. Two amber beams, (20, 15.6, 6.4) W, turn the receiver on; when one of them stops, the remaining (10, 7.8, 3.2) W holds it; from dark, one amber beam never turns it on.
- **Cone light.** One receiver law serves every kind of light (owner decision 9). The Flashlight emits (30, 30, 30) W over a 15° half-angle: solid angle Ω = 2π(1 − cos 15°) = 0.2141 sr, so 90 / 0.2141 = 420.4 W/sr in total and 140.1 W/sr per channel. With its lens 3 m in front of the disc on the disc's axis, facing it, the irradiance is 420.4 / 3² = 46.7 W/m²; the disc's share is 0.950 m² × 46.7 W/m² = 44.4 W, 14.8 W per channel (it is the only receiver in the cone and 44.4 W is below the 90 W emitted, so no share is scaled): strength 14.8 W, 14.8 / 2.5 = 5.9 times the default, matched (row R11). A laser row is never proof of torch reception ([sequence-task-398](../requirements.md#sequence-task-398)).
- **Finite disc.** A ray 0.50 m off-centre is read; one 0.60 m off-centre meets the plate.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The switched output feeds any `PowerIn`; the proof load is the Powered gate. A contact consumes no power, so the Battery's 120 W default limit is unaffected by the receiver; three lasers draw 36 W together. The 0.26 m plate is a wall to every ball.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. No optical solve is delivered today. The strength committed with one tick is read by the node `matched` at the start of the next, so light closes the supplied contact one tick later, as a delivered state-following contact does (`LatchSupplyFixtureTests.SetClosesRouteOneTickAfterStrikeWithoutTopologyChange`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/LatchSupplyFixtureTests.cs:145`: the route closes one tick after the state sets). All readings of a tick commit together, so the three contributions of a tick are never read apart. This is the requirement's "trace every emitter, then commit all receiver readings together before the electrical solve" ([todo-290](../requirements.md#todo-290)), where the solve is the one that consumes them.

### Engine conformance (22 November 2026)

Checked against the owner's engine-conformance instruction ([element-free engine design](../../../_bmad/custom/element-free-engine-design.md); AGENTS.md, Tri-Graph architecture). A search of `engine/`, `ui/` and the `CuriousContraptions.*` runtime projects for the 22 Batch E package ids found no reference; only `tools/Coverage` requirement tables name them. Verdict: **Conforms**: the declaration needs no element logic and every missing capability has one owning element-free story.

1. **Declaration data only.** A scene, four colliders, one aperture, one receiver rule, one threshold signal node, one contact node, two sockets, one parameter, three bindings. The legacy channel `switch`, per-part reception callback and parameter dictionary (harvest facts 2, 4 and 5) are not carried; "white" is declared data (the required set {R, G, B} and a purity fraction), never an engine branch on a colour or a part. White and Broadband are distinguished by their declared strength rule (Minimum against WeightedSum), not by an enum case in the engine.
2. **Generic laws.** Transport and absorption (optical transport law); summation of all light at one aperture; strength (the receiver rule with Minimum over the required set {R, G, B} and purity fraction 0.9); `matched` (the signal network's threshold rule with release ratio 0.9); switching (the delivered signal-state supply contact). Each is a general capability any package may declare; the eight receivers are one rule with eight sets of values.
3. **Three layers.** Colliders: two boxes and two spheres on the static body; nothing in physics reads art. Visuals: four solids and nine flat prints; no art node is a collider. Animation: three colour bindings read the committed signal level `matched` and write nothing back.
4. **One-way flow and typing.** Optics → committed reading and strength → the signal node at the start of the next tick → the supply contact; committed state → presentation. No contact reads the receiver directly. `PortId`, `PortRole`, `SimulationDomain` and the planned response, aggregation and channel-set types are typed; all values are f32.

**Tolerances** (the optical family's one set; coordinator decision 21). Power readings, beam or cone, ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 or 0.5 factor is at least 10 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Threshold cases are placed 5 % either side of the reading (7 % in R3b): these are positions of designed cases, not tolerances, and those rows assert state only. Exact: typed refusals, authored Reset values and colour endpoints, and a zero reading where no light reaches the aperture (declared limits and intentional zero). No row sits within 2 % of a threshold.

## Open questions

All resolved. Needs the owner: none.

1. **Amber satisfies White at the default — decided 22 November 2026 under the standing delegation: intended.** Reason: the rule asks for all three channels at once, from one source or several; amber carries all three (decision D18), and the requirement row says "not exact spectral matching". A level that must force a three-beam mix sets `threshold` above 3.2 W (row R4 proves 5 W). The Flashlight, the white source (decision K-9), satisfies this receiver by the one receiver law (owner decision 9; row R11).
2. **The 90 % set rule — decided: kept declared, with no other meaning.** With every channel required the rule cannot reject; it is declared so that all seven colour members carry one rule shape and differ only in the required set (R5 proves it never rejects).
3. **Threshold range — decided:** 0.5 to 20 W, default 2.5 W, as every receiver in the family (decision K-1: one legacy unit is 10 W), applied to each channel. Reason: [todo-301](../requirements.md#todo-301) says "every named channel at threshold"; one range for one rule keeps the family a family (D19).
4. **Tick order — coordinator decision 16; conventions item 4.** The order is in Behaviour and numbers.
5. **Pure sources.** Their radiant power, 10 W in one channel for a demand of 12 W, is the value of EL-176 to EL-178 and of the [optical conventions](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#optical-conventions).
6. **Decided 22 November 2026 under the standing delegation.** (a) Target, ring, centre and channel bars are flat prints; reason: visible = solid, and a relief of a few centimetres changes no puzzle outcome. (b) The base is raised 0.09 m and the sockets move to the plate's side faces; reason: the legacy geometry left a floating plate and buried studs. (c) One bar format for the family (0.08 × 0.04 m, rows 0.05 m apart, centred on y 0.625), set by this member's three rows; reason: the legacy rows span y 0.42 to 0.78, over the target and past the plate top. (d) Release ratio 0.9 (decision K-4): `matched` turns off below 0.9 × `threshold`; reason: a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact. 7. **Coupling — coordinator decision 16; conventions item 9.** Committed strength → the threshold signal node `matched` → the delivered signal-state contact. No contact reads the receiver.
