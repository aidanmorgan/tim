### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** Optical transport delivers channel power to the aperture; the receiver rule turns the reading into a committed strength; the threshold signal node turns the strength into the level `matched`; the supply network's contact law passes whatever supply reaches `PowerIn` to `Supply` while `matched` is on. The part adds no law of its own.
- **Default threshold 2.5 W** (0.25 legacy units; [requirement row](../requirements.md#current-cat-038); DESIGN.md: "accepting one half-power amber beam but not one quarter-power branch"). The amber laser emits (10, 7.8, 3.2) W: mean (10 + 7.8 + 3.2) / 3 = 7.0 W. One split halves it: 3.5 W ≥ 2.5 W, lit, 40 % above. Two splits: 1.75 W, dark, 30 % below the threshold and 22 % below the 2.25 W release level. After one mirror: 7.0 × 0.95 = 6.65 W, lit.
- **Range 0.5 to 20 W.** At 0.5 W an amber beam still lights after three splits (7.0 / 8 = 0.875 W) and not after four (0.4375 W, below the 0.45 W release level too). At 20 W, three amber beams on one disc light it (3 × 7.0 = 21.0 W ≥ 20 W) and two do not (14.0 W, below the 18 W release level): readings sum. Three amber lasers ask 3 × 25 = 75 W of the default Battery's 120 W, so each emits at full power.
- **Release ratio 0.9** (decision K-4 of [readiness/batch-k.md](readiness/batch-k.md): a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact). A matched receiver stays matched until its strength falls below 0.9 × `threshold`; a dark one needs the full `threshold`. With `threshold` 7.37 W the band runs from 0.9 × 7.37 = 6.63 W to 7.37 W, and one amber beam (7.0 W) lies inside it: 5 % below the threshold and 5.5 % above release. Two amber beams (14.0 W) turn the receiver on; when one of them stops, the remaining 7.0 W holds it; from dark, 7.0 W never turns it on.
- **Cone light.** One receiver law serves every kind of light (owner decision 9). The Flashlight emits (30, 30, 30) W over a 15° half-angle: solid angle 2π(1 − cos 15°) = 0.2141 sr, so 90 / 0.2141 = 420.4 W/sr in total and 140.1 W/sr per channel. With its lens 3 m in front of the disc on the disc's axis, facing it, the irradiance is 420.4 / 3² = 46.7 W/m²; the disc's share is 0.950 m² × 46.7 W/m² = 44.4 W, 14.8 W per channel (it is the only receiver in the cone and 44.4 W is below the 90 W emitted, so no scaling applies): mean 14.8 W, lit. The mean falls to 2.5 W at d = √(0.950 × 140.1 / 2.5) = 7.3 m, inside the torch's 8 m range. Row R11 runs in this slice when the Flashlight (CAT-029, Story 13.1) is delivered first, as the story order has it, and is otherwise added by the Flashlight slice. A laser row is never proof of torch reception ([sequence-task-398](../requirements.md#sequence-task-398)).
- **Finite disc.** Radius 0.55 m on a 1.4 m plate: a ray 0.50 m off-centre is read; one 0.60 m off-centre meets the plate and is occluded.
- **Nearest absorber.** An absorbed ray ends, so of two receivers in line only the nearer reads it.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The switched output feeds any `PowerIn`: the proof load is the Powered gate (delivered). A contact passes power and consumes none, so the Battery's 120 W default limit is unaffected by the receiver. The 0.26 m plate is a wall to every ball; a ball in the beam is an occluder.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. No optical solve is delivered today. The strength committed with one tick is read by the node `matched` at the start of the next, so light closes the supplied contact one tick later, as a delivered state-following contact does (`LatchSupplyFixtureTests.SetClosesRouteOneTickAfterStrikeWithoutTopologyChange`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/LatchSupplyFixtureTests.cs:145`: the route closes one tick after the state sets). This is the requirement's "trace every emitter, then commit all receiver readings together before the electrical solve" ([todo-290](../requirements.md#todo-290)), where the solve is the one that consumes them.

**Tolerances** (the optical family's one set; coordinator decision 21). Power readings, beam or cone, ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 or 0.5 factor is at least 10 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Threshold cases are placed 5 % either side of the strength: these are positions of designed cases, not tolerances, and those rows assert state only. Exact: typed refusals, authored Reset values and colour endpoints, and a zero reading where no light reaches the aperture (declared limits and intentional zero). No row sits within 2 % of a threshold.

**Rows owed to the Laser (CAT-036), run in this slice.** `LightReceiverTests.TheDetectorLightsWithTheBeamAndSuppliesOnlyWithItsOwnSupply` (theory, eight rows): over the eight combinations of Laser enable, Laser supply and receiver supply, the receiver is matched iff the Laser is enabled and supplied, and the gate is supplied iff all three hold. `LightReceiverTests.AReceiverBeyondTheLaserRangeStaysDark`: a target 15.9 m from the lens is lit and one at 16.1 m is dark (0.1 m either side of the 16 m range).
