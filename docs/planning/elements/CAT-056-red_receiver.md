### Behaviour and numbers

- **Law.** Optical transport delivers channel power to the aperture; the receiver rule turns the reading into a committed strength (zero when purity fails); the threshold signal node turns the strength into the level `matched`; the supply network's contact law passes whatever supply reaches `PowerIn` to `Supply` while `matched` is on. The part adds no law of its own.
- **Pure red light.** The red laser (EL-176) emits (10, 0, 0) W for a demand of 12 W: strength 10 W ≥ 2.5 W (0.25 legacy units), purity 10 / 10 = 100 %, matched. Red-filtered amber is the same (10, 0, 0) W. One split: 5 W, lit, twice the default. Three splits: 1.25 W, dark, half the default and 44 % below the 2.25 W release level. (Two splits give exactly 2.5 W, so no row uses them.)
- **Wrong colour.** A green beam (0, 10, 0) W or a blue beam (0, 0, 10) W has R = 0: dark.
- **Amber.** The amber laser (10, 7.8, 3.2) W has R = 10 W, four times the threshold, but purity 10 / 21 = 47.6 % < 90 %: dark. That a strong, impure beam fails is the lesson (decision D18).
- **Purity limit.** Matched needs the other channels together to be at most R / 9. With one red beam the limit is 10 / 9 = 1.11 W. A second beam of blue-filtered amber (0, 0, 3.2) W brought in through two splitters adds 3.2 / 4 = 0.8 W: purity 10 / 10.8 = 92.6 %, matched (28 % under the limit). Through one splitter it adds 1.6 W: purity 10 / 11.6 = 86.2 %, dark (44 % over the limit). Purity has no release band: it is tested on every tick, and strength is zero in a tick where it fails.
- **Threshold range 0.5 to 20 W.** At 0.5 W a red beam still lights after four splits (0.625 W) and not after five (0.3125 W, below the 0.45 W release level too). At 20 W, three red beams on one target light it (30 W) and one does not (10 W, below the 18 W release level): readings sum. Three red lasers ask 3 × 12 = 36 W of the default Battery's 120 W, so each emits at full power.
- **Release ratio 0.9** (decision K-4 of [readiness/batch-k.md](readiness/batch-k.md): a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact). A matched receiver stays matched until its strength falls below 0.9 × `threshold`; a dark one needs the full `threshold`. With `threshold` 10.5 W the band runs from 0.9 × 10.5 = 9.45 W to 10.5 W, and one red beam (10 W) lies inside it: 4.8 % below the threshold and 5.8 % above release. Two red beams (20 W) turn the receiver on; when one of them stops, the remaining 10 W holds it; from dark, 10 W never turns it on.
- **Cone light.** One receiver law serves every kind of light (owner decision 9). The Flashlight emits (30, 30, 30) W over a 15° half-angle: solid angle Ω = 2π(1 − cos 15°) = 0.2141 sr, so 90 / 0.2141 = 420.4 W/sr in total and 30 / 0.2141 = 140.1 W/sr per channel. With its lens 3 m in front of the disc on the disc's axis, facing it, the irradiance is 420.4 / 3² = 46.7 W/m² (140.1 / 9 = 15.57 W/m² per channel); the disc's share is 0.950 m² × 46.7 W/m² = 44.4 W, 0.950 × 140.1 / 9 = 14.8 W per channel (it is the only receiver in the cone and 44.4 W is below the 90 W emitted, so no share is scaled). Red is 14.8 / 2.5 = 5.9 times the threshold, but purity is 14.8 / 44.4 = 33.3 %: strength zero, dark (row R11). With a Red filter (CAT-055, transmittance (1, 0, 0)) on the axis midway, the cone is multiplied by the pane (conventions item 3) and the reading is (14.8, 0, 0) W: purity 100 %, strength 14.8 W, 5.9 times the default, matched (row R11b). At 1.5 m the cone's radius is 1.5 × tan 15° = 0.40 m, inside the pane's 0.65 m half-width, so the frame shades no sample. Row R11 runs in this slice when the Flashlight (CAT-029, Story 13.1) is delivered first, as the story order has it, and is otherwise added by the Flashlight slice; row R11b runs in the Colour filters slice (Story 13.4), which delivers after this one. A laser row is never proof of torch reception ([sequence-task-398](../requirements.md#sequence-task-398)).
- **Finite disc.** A ray 0.50 m off-centre is read; one 0.60 m off-centre meets the plate.
- **Fit with delivered parts.** The switched output feeds any `PowerIn`; the proof load is the Powered gate. A contact consumes no power, so the Battery's 120 W default limit is unaffected. The 0.26 m plate is a wall to every ball.
- **Tick order.** One optical and radiation solve per committed tick of duration dt, after the last substep and before the thermal step and commit (the [combined tick order](EL-176-red-laser-emitter.md#combined-tick-order), optical convention 4; coordinator decisions 16 and 26). It reads the energy granted to each emitter over this tick's substeps, this tick's final collider poses and signal levels as resolved at the start of this tick. Segments, readings and poses commit together; readings act from the next tick. No row asserts a tick except where a next-tick decision is the feature. No optical solve is delivered today. The strength committed with one tick is read by the node `matched` at the start of the next, so light closes the supplied contact one tick later, as a delivered state-following contact does (`LatchSupplyFixtureTests.SetClosesRouteOneTickAfterStrikeWithoutTopologyChange`, `CuriousContraptions.tests/LatchSupplyFixtureTests.cs:145`: the route closes one tick after the state sets). This is the requirement's "trace every emitter, then commit all receiver readings together before the electrical solve" ([todo-290](../requirements.md#todo-290)), where the solve is the one that consumes them.

**Tolerances** (the optical family's one set; coordinator decision 21). Power readings, beam or cone, ± 2 %: the smallest declared loss step is 5 % (one mirror), so 2 % tells n lossy parts from n ± 1, and a missing 0.9 or 0.5 factor is at least 10 %; a wrong factor flips a detector. "Below 2 % of the passed channel" behind a filter is the same bound, so a pane that leaks a channel fails. Stall guard 1 s of game time: it excludes a light that never arrives; the response itself is one tick. Threshold cases are placed 5 % either side of the reading: these are positions of designed cases, not tolerances, and those rows assert state only. Exact: typed refusals, authored Reset values and colour endpoints, and a zero reading where no light reaches the aperture (declared limits and intentional zero). No row sits within 2 % of a threshold, and no contaminant within 2 % of its purity limit.

5. **Decided 22 November 2026 under the standing delegation.** (a) Target, ring, centre and channel bar are flat prints; reason: visible = solid, and a relief of a few centimetres changes no puzzle outcome. (b) The base is raised 0.09 m and the sockets move to the plate's side faces; reason: the legacy geometry left a floating plate and buried studs. (c) One bar format for the family (0.08 × 0.04 m, rows 0.05 m apart, centred on y 0.625); reason: the legacy rows, 0.13 m apart and 0.1 m high, overhang the plate top and overlap the target once there are two or three rows and they are printed flat. (d) Release ratio 0.9 (decision K-4): `matched` turns off below 0.9 × `threshold`; reason: a blade or shadow edge crossing the disc must not chatter a supplied contact. (e) Palette order 612 (coordinator decision 36).
6. **Flashlight cones — owner decision 9.** One receiver law serves all light: a diverging segment adds its conserved share A × Σ wᵢEᵢ to the reading (conventions item 8). An unfiltered torch fails purity (row R11); a red-filtered torch matches (row R11b).
7. **Coupling — coordinator decision 16; conventions item 9.** Committed strength → the threshold signal node `matched` → the delivered signal-state contact. No contact reads the receiver.
