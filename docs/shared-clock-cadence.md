# Shared clock and configurable subsystem cadence

This is the current design for the common clock that paces the three systems fixed by the [compilation model](gpu-f32-physics.md#compilation-model): the physics worker at a fixed 120 Hz tick with 480 Hz substeps, the animation worker at its own rate (currently 60 Hz) and the main-thread renderer following the display at 30–60 FPS (with high-refresh display support up to 144 Hz). It replaces independent elapsed-time accumulation as scheduling authority; existing evidence remains exact to its recorded configuration. Apply the [engine lifecycle and clock bounds](engine-contracts.md#clocks-histories-and-performance), [canonical IEEE-754 f32 authority](gpu-f32-physics.md), P0-016/P0-024/P0-025/P0-026 and REQ-09/REQ-14 together. No threshold, workload or precision exception is introduced.

Exact mapping/feedback-age baselines, animation diagnostic extensions and controlled 100/250 ms stall campaigns are [release-checklist items](delivery-workflow.md#stage-gates) owned by P0-032/P0-034 and repeated at P0-035 engine closure, not prerequisites of a playable slice; ordinary Run/Pause/Step/Resume/Reset correctness in Chrome remains required per slice.

**Development timing relaxation:** Fixed-step timing budgets (<0.5 ms solver time, 2.5 ms tick p95, zero clock-debt over 20 runs, display-rate pacing) are target recommendations and advisory telemetry during incremental development slices; they are not blocking pass/fail gates for functional slices. Formal qualification is enforced at the P0-034 stage gate.

## One authority and independently executing consumers

The Simulation host owns one session master clock, initialized once from its admitted monotonic native clock. Its nonzero session and clock generation identify the origin. It continues while the world is Building, Paused or Completed; it is not the committed physics tick and never restarts for Run or Reset.

Browser and Animation retain native clocks only as calibrated measurement/wakeup adapters to this master. They derive due work locally from the admitted master-time interval and immutable schedule; they do not wait for a Browser message on every pulse. Simulation clock/control service remains independent of awaiting worker dispatch. There is no additional clock worker or broadcast-per-pulse queue. A lost master/session faults or retires the session under the existing policy; no consumer elects a substitute authority.

Native origin mapping retains eight-probe qualification, current uncertainty/drift/expiry/recovery bounds and no shrinking retained uncertainty. Every mapped scheduling decision carries the matching session, clock generation and cadence revision. Animation maps to the same Simulation master; its mapping uncertainty must satisfy the existing end-to-end bound, not receive another additive allowance. The exact clock-control routes and their existing lease accounting must be specified in the forward codec before worker cutover.

## Exact pulses and bounded missed work

For admitted rate p/q Hz, pulse n has exact master-relative time n*q/p seconds. At integer master elapsed nanoseconds e, the due ordinal is floor(e*p/(1,000,000,000*q)), using checked widened integer products. Its first eligible integer timestamp is ceil(n*1,000,000,000*q/p). Calculate from the original epoch and ordinal, never by repeatedly adding a rounded period. A pulse exactly on its boundary is due; an earlier one is not. Non-divisor schedules such as 144 Hz presentation with 120 Hz simulation require no common rounded period or giant least-common-multiple timer.

For a consumer whose mapped master time is an interval, use its lower bound to authorize a due pulse; retain the interval for freshness evidence. Timers and display callbacks only wake this calculation. Service return/ack/control traffic before bounded work. At most four whole simulation ticks run per turn. Every due physical tick is executed in order; no skipped tick, larger dt or discarded debt. More than 100 ms of physical-time debt still stops with Capacity.

Animation evaluates at most once per turn at its latest due ordinal, preserving reliable occurrences and their exact world identity. Superseded cosmetic evaluation opportunities may coalesce under that existing policy; they are reported, not presented as executed frames. Presentation applies at most once per actual display callback, choosing the latest eligible presentation ordinal. Missed opportunities never fabricate displayed frames. Lower device refresh than the selected target remains observable missed cadence and fails applicable release-checklist admission, rather than changing the selected frequency silently.

## Active defaults and internal cadence configuration

The active Workshop uses Simulation 120 Hz, Animation 60 Hz and Presentation 60 Hz on the same master. These defaults live in one typed configuration owner and are not exposed as player controls. Preserve the internal settings/protocol and parameterized schedules so future development can change rates without another clock architecture. Rates and ordinals have distinct typed values; strings appear only at serialization/external boundaries. Internal capability and current defaults are:

| Setting | Admitted choices | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simulation commit cadence | 60, 120, 240 Hz | 120 Hz |
| Physical integration profile | Canonical 480 Hz f32 profile | 480 Hz |
| Animation evaluation cadence | 30, 60, 90, 120 Hz | 60 Hz |
| Presentation cadence | 30, 60, 90, 120, 144 Hz, or admitted display refresh | 60 Hz |

Alternate internal rates are future configuration capability, not active player options or a prerequisite to qualifying the current 120/60/60 defaults. Changing a default requires the affected numerical, timing, lifecycle and UI qualification; preserved alternate-rate evidence remains scoped to its exact inputs. Display refresh is an explicit admitted external rate captured in the schedule; subsequent refresh changes require re-admission and cannot create a second time authority. A fixed presentation selection remains fixed. Unsupported/undefined selectors, nonpositive rates, overflow and incompatible physical ratios reject atomically.

Simulation frequency changes real transactions and commits, not merely publication thinning. Each commit executes exactly 480/F physical substeps of the [solver model](gpu-f32-physics.md#solver-model): eight at 60 Hz, four at 120 Hz, two at 240 Hz. The physical profile remains exact rational 1/480 second. A substep ordinal is commitOrdinal*substepsPerCommit plus its local substep, with checked arithmetic and explicit profile identity; admission, contact/sensor bounds and host validators consume that same value. Canonical stored velocity units remain unchanged. Element declarations supply parameters to that shared path; no catalogue-specific law table exists.

No 60/240 Hz Pass is inferred from 120 Hz evidence. A different physical integration frequency is a separately declared numerical profile requiring its own f32 dt, time/phase equations, operand bounds and qualification; this design does not fake that change by adjusting a timer.

The Run limit is 30 seconds of committed world time: 1800/3600/7200 commits at the admitted rates. Replay/diagnostic storage must explicitly cover the admitted maximum including the initial record; its state width and all metadata/managed/wire quotas are recomputed from the actual forward ABI rather than silently truncated or reused from an old record cap. Reliable command tick identities stay exact; any tick-count capacity, lookahead or timeout is explicitly distinguished from physical duration before changing it.

## SharedArrayBuffer Pose Ring, History Capacity and Ceilings

To eliminate GPU-to-CPU readback stalls (`mapAsync` stalls of 0.5–2 ms) and thread-to-thread copying overhead, the simulation worker publishes committed body poses directly into a lock-free zero-copy triple-buffered ring in `SharedArrayBuffer`.

Each buffer slot in the ring stores contiguous SIMD-aligned 32-bit floats for all dynamic and kinematic bodies (position xyz, orientation quaternion xyzw, linear velocity xyz, flags), preceded by a 64-bit atomic sequence counter and a 64-bit committed timestamp.

The atomic sequence protocol ensures torn reads are impossible without mutex locks:
1. **Writer Protocol (Simulation Worker):**
   - Atomically increment the sequence counter from $2k$ to $2k + 1$ (odd, indicating write in progress).
   - Write contiguous pose floats using 128-bit SIMD stores.
   - Atomically increment the sequence counter to $2k + 2$ (even, indicating write committed).
2. **Reader Protocol (Browser Presentation Adapter):**
   - Read the slot's sequence counter via `Atomics.load`. If odd, wait or select the alternative stable buffer.
   - Read the pose data.
   - Re-read the sequence counter via `Atomics.load`. If it does not match the initial even value, the read was raced; retry with the alternative buffer.

The presentation adapter samples bracketing committed substep snapshots from the ring and evaluates Slerp interpolation for orientation and linear interpolation for position at the coherent display timestamp. Missing or out-of-order samples retain the prior coherent frame; no extrapolation across missing data is permitted. This ring guarantees sub-millisecond, zero-copy presentation updates at display refresh rates from 30 Hz to 144 Hz.

## World time, autonomous UI and lifecycle

The world derives a running-time projection from the single master clock. Run establishes a world epoch and an admitted master-time start after its required recipient barrier, with tick zero at that start and the first whole tick due exactly 1/F later. This world lattice is epoch-relative; it never borrows an arbitrary next session pulse and advances a whole dt over a shorter first interval. Autonomous UI/animation and presentation schedules retain the session lattice. Committed world time is exactly n/F. Pause requires the exact session, world generation, cadence revision and projection, but permits a newer physical authority revision: queued ticks may commit before its arrival. It drains the owned in-flight tick, then freezes this world projection at the acknowledged committed endpoint. Resume and Step retain exact authority-revision admission in the paused world. Resume installs an acknowledged master-time anchor at the frozen committed n/F, with the next whole tick due exactly 1/F later, excluding paused elapsed time. Step advances exactly one configured simulation tick while paused, including its complete physical substeps, and completes only after the matching endpoint is displayed. It does not advance autonomous UI by an invented physics delta.

Autonomous UI and UI animation sample continuous master session time. World-dependent animation and physical presentation use the same selected committed world timestamp, with existing fixed display delay, coherent brackets and no extrapolation. They must not substitute an independently accumulated frame delta. Reset advances world generation, restores captured construction/configuration, retires old world histories and reseeds tick zero; it does not restart master time or reset an unrelated UI lifetime. Completed holds its matching endpoint while master-driven UI can continue. Visibility/suspension retains the existing acknowledged Pause/recalibration/reseed policy.

The Workshop exposes Run, Pause, Resume, Step and Reset, but no frequency selectors or Apply-frequency action. Future cadence changes use the existing typed internal configuration boundary, not a parallel setting mechanism. Internal configuration installs only in acknowledged Building state. The host validates the full immutable settings, resources and profile, advances a nonreused cadence revision, and admits matching schedules in all required recipients before acknowledging application or allowing Run. Pending work/results from an older cadence revision cannot mutate the new schedule. Any precommit rejection leaves all old settings/schedules intact; postcommit recipient loss follows the existing Indeterminate/fault policy. Running, Paused and Completed reconfiguration rejects explicitly. No mid-Run frequency mutation, alias, migration or silent rebase is supported.

## Forward clock/configuration boundary

The first implementation uses these exact little-endian layouts. All enum values are declared in C# and emitted at the JS/WASM worker boundary. Unknown versions, undefined values, nonzero reserved fields, stale identities, wrong routes and trailing/truncated bytes reject before mutation. No old-format decoder or implicit configuration default remains.

**Typed settings, 32 bytes.** Offsets: schema U32 at 0 (version 1); SimulationCadence U32 at 4 (60/120/240 Hz map to 1/2/3); PhysicalStepProfile U32 at 8 (Canonical480Hz=1); AnimationCadence U32 at 12 (30/60/90/120 map to 1/2/3/4); PresentationCadence U32 at 16 (AdmittedDisplay=0, fixed 30/60/90/120/144 map to 1..5); resolved presentation rate numerator U32 at 20 and denominator U32 at 24; reserved U32 at 28. The resolved positive reduced rational must equal the fixed selection where fixed, or the separately admitted display rate where display-driven. Limit numerator<=240000, denominator<=10000 and 30<=p/q<=240; validation uses integer products. Settings contain no game-value float or encoded behavior string.

**Master peer, 64 bytes; clock wire version 3.** Version U32 at 0, admitted native precision profile U32 at 4, session128 at 8, master clock generation U64 at 24, master native origin I64 at 32, requester native generation U64 at 40, timestamp uncertainty U64 at 48, requester role U32 at 56, master role U32 at 60 (Simulation only). Roles are Browser=1, Simulation=2, Animation=3. Origin and all native stamps are nonnegative. A probe appends sequence U64 at 64 and requester native sent I64 at 72 (80 bytes); reply appends master native received/sent I64 at 80/88 (96 bytes). The master origin subtracts from mapped native time to yield typed master elapsed time. No browser epoch is authority. Existing probe/recovery bounds and exact identity checks apply independently to B→S and A→S; these are clock-control routes, not new animation gameplay command routes.

Clock diagnostic records are 144 bytes: exact reply 0..95, requester receipt I64 at 96, outcome U32 at 104, received length U32 at 108, offset lower/upper I128 at 112/128. The existing 128-entry clock ring remains bounded (18,432 payload bytes); collection capacities are recomputed from the declared run/cadence, not silently truncated.

**Reliable schedule control.** A 64-byte header carries schema U32 at 0 (version 1), typed kind U32 at 4 (Prepare=1, Prepared=2, Commit=3, Applied=4, Abort=5, Presented=6), session128 at 8, master generation U64 at 24, proposed cadence revision U64 at 32, projection epoch U64 at 40, sender/recipient role U32 at 48/52, payload length U32 at 56 and reserved U32 at 60. Only S coordinates installation; B/A reply to S. Prepare has settings32, expected old cadence/projection U64 at 32/40, typed transition U32 at 48 (Configure=1, Run=2, Pause=3, Resume=4, Step=5, Completed=6, Reset=7), reserved U32 at 52, target world generation U64 at 56, target world tick U64 at 64, endpoint publication identity U64 at 72, and the complete prepared endpoint at 80. Zero publication is permitted only for a nonpublication acknowledgement seed under its existing shape rule. Prepared/Applied/Presented carry final applied master-sample upper timestamp I64 and outcome/reason U32 at 8/12 (16 bytes). An upper integer timestamp conservatively rounds the exact rational sample upward, so installation cannot rewind by fractional-nanosecond truncation. Abort carries reason U32 and reserved U32 (8 bytes).

Commit carries a 144-byte schedule: settings 0..31; cadence revision U64 at 32; master generation U64 at 40; master native origin I64 at 48; install master elapsed I64 at 56; first Animation/Presentation ordinal U64 at 64/72; prior final Animation/Presentation master sample I64 at 80/88; world generation U64 at 96; projection anchor world tick U64 at 104; projection anchor master elapsed I64 at 112; typed world playback U32 at 120; reserved U32 at 124; projection epoch U64 at 128; reserved U64 at 136. World playback maps Building/Running/Paused/Completed/Faulted to 0..4. Header/payload identities must agree.

World generation, projection epoch and cadence revision are distinct. Reset/Load retire the world generation. Run/Pause/Resume/Step/Completed transitions advance the projection epoch without treating an old endpoint capture as a new capture. The explicit projection anchor is not a physics capture stamp: retained tick-zero/paused endpoints preserve their original capture; history reseeds only at the matching admitted transition.

**Configuration transaction.** B sends a reliable ConfigureCadence request only in Building. S validates the full candidate, exact expected revision/profile and resource reservations; there is one pending installation. Prepare quiesces old cadence evaluations in B/A and reserves their new schedule/history state; input/acks remain serviceable. Recipients report their final applied master times. Rejection/timeout before commit aborts all prepared candidates and preserves the old configuration. Abort reopens each old schedule at its current due ordinal, never below its retained applied time; it does not replay skipped cosmetic opportunities. Published proposed revisions are never reused even after Abort. S then commits its prevalidated settings/world candidate, installs a fresh cadence revision and issues Commit. Recipients install exact matching state and reply Applied; only then is the settings command acknowledged Applied and Run enabled. A postcommit failure is Indeterminate/fault under the existing lifecycle contract, not rollback or automatic retry.

For each session-lattice consumer, firstOrdinal = floor(max(installMasterTime, priorFinalAppliedMasterTime)*p/(1e9*q))+1. No ordinal below this floor is eligible under the new revision; late delivery may evaluate the latest due ordinal only if it is at least firstOrdinal. This prevents a 120→30 Hz change at 50 ms from replaying 33.333 ms after 41.667 ms. Run/Resume retain their separately declared epoch-relative first whole tick at anchor+1/F. A delayed commit never invents shortened physical dt.

**Bootstrap and owned clock route.** B creates one session and starts S and A with their exact role/build/schema identities. S admits its native clock and captures master origin once before issuing the authoritative bootstrap; generation starts at 1. B transfers the two endpoints of one MessageChannel exactly once to S and A and retains neither endpoint. S owns one port, A owns the other; session disposal/crash closes both, retires the master generation and prevents old-port reuse. The route admits only typed Probe, Reply and ScheduleControl envelopes; S accepts A-origin probes and Prepared/Applied replies, A accepts S replies and Prepare/Commit/Abort. It does not accept animation gameplay requests, physical mutations or arbitrary callbacks. B↔S retains its existing owned worker channel. B and A each complete independent 8-probe mapping qualification against S before initial schedule installation/Ready. Native clock profile or route failure rejects startup; there is no Browser-clock substitute.

**Projection transitions.** Every Workshop request also names its expected projection epoch; stale cadence/projection rejects before preparation. S serializes one lifecycle transition. Run prepares the existing tick-zero seed; Pause prevents new ticks and settles the current candidate before selecting its last committed endpoint; Resume prepares that unchanged paused endpoint; Step prepares exactly one simulation tick candidate while paused; Completed uses the already committed terminal endpoint; Reset prepares new-world tick-zero admission. Each Prepare identifies the candidate projection/endpoint without publishing it as current. Failed Step/Reset preparation discards its candidate and preserves authority; failure cannot fabricate a capture stamp.

After all matching Prepared replies, S commits prevalidated world/projection assignments and publishes the matching schedule Commit before any new-projection read. B/A install their reserved seed/history and schedule together, acknowledge Applied, and reject old projection work. Reads/results remain bounded behind this lifecycle barrier; no arbitrary wait queue is added. Run/Resume anchors are chosen from the actual S master at commit, with the first tick due one full configured period later. Step remains Paused (or Completed); its command completion additionally waits for Browser Presented at the matching endpoint after actual property application, rather than treating history admission as rendering. Completed/Reset likewise retain their required endpoint application barrier. Postcommit recipient failure remains explicit Indeterminate/fault. If precommit Pause is rejected, its old running schedule retains real elapsed debt rather than silently excluding the preparation interval.

**Bounded reservations.** The coordinator owns one active schedule, one prepared schedule, one original lifecycle request, at most two recipient Prepared/Applied results, two reserved outbound controls, and one precommit candidate. B/A each own one active/prepared schedule, one prepared endpoint and one receipt/result. Fixed per-host schedule storage reservations are S 4096 payload bytes/256 metadata bytes/1024 copy scratch and B/A 2048 payload bytes/128 metadata bytes/1024 copy scratch each; these are sub-reservations within existing host/transport ceilings, not additional total budgets or managed-heap measurements. Arrays/records and result slots are fixed-size, with no installation queue or per-pulse task. The largest control (64-byte header plus the Prepare payload carrying the complete prepared endpoint) must fit inside the 1024-byte control lease limit; recompute it from the actual endpoint width of the forward ABI. Native owner-layout/peak checks must account every actual field and retained copy within these reservations.

A Diagnostic trace has one 1 MiB raw-state reservation, serialized in fixed raw chunks with a frozen 128 KiB output-scratch reservation for the chunk, its Base64 payload and one bounded composed console payload plus headers. The encoder must avoid retaining simultaneous additional joined/copy strings outside that bound; direct chunk streaming and explicit lifetime checks are required. This scratch is distinct from state payload, counted in the Diagnostic host memory footprint, and absent from Production. No claim of complete managed occupancy follows from these payload ceilings.

**Affected current Workshop/Simulation shapes.** The Workshop command header carries expected cadence revision U64 and expected projection epoch U64; ConfigureCadence carries the 32-byte settings. Pause/Resume/Step are explicit command kinds, not Run aliases. Construction carries the canonical construction plus the 32-byte settings; ordinary construction cannot silently change settings. Cancel identity is 40 bytes: original command sequence/world generation/authority revision/cadence revision/projection epoch as five U64 values. Only initial Initialize may expect zero cadence/projection; all admitted commands require the exact active nonzero identities. Responses carry cadence revision U64, master generation U64, projection epoch U64 and source pulse ordinal U64. All consumers bind these to the installed schedule before history/ACK mutation.

Simulation scene descriptors carry SimulationCadence U32, PhysicalStepProfile U32 and cadence revision U64 after the physical lanes; descriptors of a previous layout/version reject. The simulation solver derives exact substepsPerCommit from the typed selectors and checks profile/revision; host validation uses the same product for sample age and end ordinal. Exact byte offsets, descriptor version numbers, trace record widths and the physical history's declared reservation (7 * actual sample width + 1024) are recorded in code and slice evidence under the forward ABI (P0-016), not here; old ring widths reject. Presentation diagnostics append cadence revision and selected presentation ordinal to their record.

## Forward replacement and acceptance

Before runtime edits, independently freeze exact configuration/pulse/clock message fields and versions, owner/commit ordering, bounded storage, and current call-site changes. Replace the private Run Stopwatch, hardcoded tick/120 conversions, tick*4 assumptions, local UI delta accumulation and independent animation scheduling; no old scheduling path remains reachable. Browser property application remains on the browser; animation evaluation belongs in the independent Animation worker.

Required proof for the active 120/60/60 defaults: integer/rational endpoint and overflow controls, atomic settings/old-revision rejection, actual two-worker Workshop without frequency controls, unchanged 120 Hz same-time physics/energy behaviour inside the [game-grade envelope](gpu-f32-physics.md#game-grade-envelope), Pause/Step/Resume/Reset and UI activity while world time is paused, independent-worker progress, missed pulses/debt and delayed delivery, exact construction restoration and save/load where supported. Future changes to the internal configuration additionally require affected non-divisor/cross-rate and same-time profile checks before activation; compare simulation rates at equal physical times, not equal tick numbers. Existing alternate-rate evidence remains exact to its recorded inputs. Preserve the 120 Hz/four-substep numerical baseline and report newly affected outcomes independently.

Production build, actual Chrome and ordinary lifecycle remain required for each admitted configuration. Release budgets, injected 100/250 ms stalls and synthetic cross-rate matrices are [release-checklist items](delivery-workflow.md#stage-gates); synthetic 144 Hz scheduling is not actual display qualification.
