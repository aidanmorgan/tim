## Implemented

- Physical open-muzzle annular barrel, solid breech and fixed navy base. Cream collars and transparent cyan chamber keep the actual ball visible. Gold charge bar, ready flag and an original toolbox icon.
- Separate typed electrical PowerIn and activation Trigger input. Finite store starts empty, charges at authored power and requires full capacity before firing. Trigger adds no charge.
- A single fully contained, slow payload of radius 0.30–0.42 is eligible. Overflow/ambiguous occupancy, unseated or moving loads are not fired. No invisible holding joint or positional snap.
- Trigger pulses coalesce until the next fixed-tick boundary. Occupancy is evaluated then, after all bodies completed the prior step; one fixed tick is the only pending window. A rejected empty/uncharged request is discarded, not remembered until a later load becomes ready.
- Finite-radius sweep checks the path through and just beyond the muzzle against other world geometry and bodies. It excludes only the cannon and the selected physical payload. An obstruction retains charge.
- Successful release modifies the existing payload velocity along the rotated barrel axis, preserving transverse motion. The anchored base absorbs momentum reaction. Energy comes from the finite store; actual added axial kinetic energy is debited. A departing payload cannot be fired again while still in the chamber.
- Refilling the chamber uses physical balls. One native test inserts a second fixture ball to isolate distinct-payload reload; another launches the existing ball vertically, lets it fall back and physically seat under gravity, recharges and fires it again without inserting or repositioning any body. A timed two-ball gate feeder is now proven below; general hopper/reload-shuttle components remain separate backlog work.
- A slate inner recoil sleeve retracts inside the fixed transparent annular jacket after accepted shots. Quintic compression/return and smooth bounded pulse combination finish even after simulation stop. The bore, breech and jacket collision geometry stay fixed; this feedback adds no payload impulse. This is anchored presentation feedback, not a free-barrel recoil simulation.
